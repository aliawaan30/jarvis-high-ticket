# How every lesson is written (locked 31 Oct 2026, Blake's rules)

Read this before writing or editing any lesson. It overrides CONVENTIONS.md where they differ.

1. First person, Blake speaking to the student. "I", "you", "we". Never "Blake does this", never "watch Blake walk through it", never "the programme" or "this course" as a third party. It is Blake writing to them. Example: "Nobody pays me twenty grand for software. They pay to stop losing more than twenty grand."
2. Simple to follow beats complete. Every lesson should make the student think "oh, this is simple." If a paragraph explains how something is built rather than what they need to understand, cut it.
3. Plain headlines. No clever headlines, no label style headings (no "THE WORKED EXAMPLE", no "Blake's rule of thumb"). Say what the section is in plain words.
4. No other coaches or gurus by name. No "the method X talks about". It is Blake's method.
5. No internal notes, file paths, agent names, handoff references or "source material" in a lesson. Students never see how the sausage is made.
6. Not all text. Where a section is a list of steps, a formula, a comparison or a process, give it a diagram (SVG in data/img, drawn simply) or a short table. Blake: "can't just be all text".
7. No forced "Do this now" sections. If there is one obvious next action, say it in one line at the end in Blake's voice ("Go film five of these today."). If the lesson is education, end on the idea, not a homework list.
8. No bot language. Blake: "you don't need to make it sound like a bot". Write how he talks: short, direct, a bit blunt. "Send it to your technical CSM to go over the offer with you" not "the scoping conversation with your implementation partner".
9. Scripts are scripts. When the student needs to say something on a call, give the full script, in order, with what to listen for. Not a card of fragments.
10. The deliverable box says what YOU will have, e.g. "By the end you will have one offer written in six lines." Not "They leave with".
11. v1 is for selling now. Clear beats perfect. Blake improves each lesson live with students and records the videos after.
12. Still true from CONVENTIONS.md: no em dashes anywhere, no dates that will age badly in the body copy unless they are in a table that says when it was captured, commercial numbers (tier prices) never appear, and the Jarvis prompts stay in b-prompt boxes.

## Added 31 Oct 2026 after Blake's second read. These are the ones we keep breaking.

13. No self narration. Never "this lesson", "the next lesson", "the last lesson", "this module", "this is the education piece", "read this twice", "bring X to the next lesson". A lesson talks about the subject, never about itself. One pointer at the very end is allowed if the student genuinely needs it ("The call one script is next.").
14. Simple ideas get simple treatment. If Blake could say it in four sentences on camera, it is four sentences. No table or diagram for a concept a tradie understands first time. Website versus landing page is the example: he shows the two pages in the video, the text is a paragraph.
15. No hedging. No "illustrative", "not a client result", "your own account is the only benchmark", "working assumptions, not promises". One plain line where a number is made up ("made up numbers, to show the shape"), nowhere else.
16. Talk, don't lecture. No citations, years, Nobel prizes, book titles. The idea in one line, in Blake's words.
17. Never restate another lesson. No "what we build in this module" lists, no tables summarising other lessons, no retelling a story already told elsewhere.
18. Titles say what you do. Doing lessons get doing titles: "Build Your Landing Page", not "Build the Page". Idea lessons get the idea: "Website vs Landing Page".
19. Headings in sentence case. Never all caps.
20. Length. An idea lesson is under 900 words. A doing lesson is under 450 words plus its prompt. A script is as long as the script. If it is longer, something above is being broken.
21. The deliverable box is gone from the student view. Keep the field short and plain for the builder; students never see it.

## Added 31 Oct 2026 after Blake's third read.

22. No nuance stacking. Blake on Build the Demo: "so many nuances to this text". One plain statement does the job. Not "rough is fine, personal to the niche is not optional, the owner needs to see their own business in it, and it protects you because...". Say the thing once and move on.
23. Explain what the thing IS before anything else. For an offer: what it is, how it works, who buys it and why, then the six lines. If a student cannot explain the offer to a mate in two sentences, the lesson has not done its job. Conviction comes from understanding, and conviction is what sells.
24. Jarvis does the work; the lesson shows the finished thing. Market scoring, offer writing, landing pages: a prompt, not a worksheet. Never a manual table for the student to fill in. Never a lesson whose only job is "get X checked".

## Added 2 Nov 2026 after Blake's fourth read.

25. Prompts are one line. Jarvis already holds the offer, the pages and the research. Never ask the student to paste in what Jarvis already knows. "Run the landing page skill for my offer. Domain: [x]." The skill asks for what is missing.
26. No "what you get back" paragraphs after a prompt, and no "read it once, leave it alone" style advice sections. Prompt, then one line on what to check.
27. Diagram text must fit the box. Bold 22 to 24px: 18 characters max in a 330px box. 18px: 28 characters. Check every label before publishing; an overflowing diagram is worse than none.
28. Lessons exist to get something done. If a lesson is a concept with no copy-paste action, it needs a reason to exist (an idea the student must hold to sell). If it is a step a technical partner or the sales call covers, it is not a lesson.

## Added 4 November after Blake’s review

29. Explain the idea directly. Avoid “four dials”, fraction directions and motivational lectures about calm certainty. Use the actual buyer result and a clear demonstration.
30. Current beginner menu has eight services. Leave pay-per-lead/pay-per-show, full-service marketing and reviews out of it. Keep advanced custom research available as an optional route.
31. A working demo is the starting proof. Do not copy another business’s 150-client authority into an AI offer.
32. Teach one complete route to launch. Skills do the jobs; learners need to understand the buyer, problem, fix and result. Pack updates preserve each member’s business records.

33. At the start of each module’s first lesson, add a short text introduction saying what the member will do, why it matters and what they will have by the end. This is the explicit exception to rules 13 and 17. Keep the existing subject teaching after it. Do not add an overview to every lesson or claim an introduction completes the practical instructions.

34. Start offer development with a niche the member understands or can access, then research its problems and choose a suitable service. Prefer actual agency service offers and relevant client cases; software deployment stories are a different kind of evidence. Generic one-job automation belongs under custom diagnosis. Recommend AI arbitrage for full custom CRMs.
