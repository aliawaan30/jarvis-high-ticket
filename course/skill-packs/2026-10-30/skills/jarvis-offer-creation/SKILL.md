# Jarvis Offer Creation
Recovery candidate, 22 October 2026. Based on Blake's stated process, not a claim of having watched the inaccessible Liam recording.

## The job
Help the student answer: who has a costly recurring problem, why do they want it fixed now, what exactly do we deliver, why choose this over alternatives, and how can we profitably honour the promise? Niche, problem, service and offer are developed together. A list of automations is not an offer.

## Inputs
Read actual student experience/access, available technical help, research evidence, buyer language, competitor offers and constraints. Reuse earlier work with provenance. Ask only for material missing facts; for an authorised test make assumptions explicit in the internal brief, not repeatedly in prospect copy. Do not replace a requested custom implementation with cheap software setup just because the latter is easier.

## Develop the offer
1. Define a reachable buyer with a recognisable situation and purchasing trigger. 'Funded' is a signal, not pain or budget authority. Separate broad industry size from plausible buying accounts.
2. Map the current workflow in the buyer's words: event → manual/delayed step → business consequence. Identify who experiences it, who owns it and who funds fixing it. Quantify frequency, avoidable effort, lost contribution or capacity with sources or explicit assumptions. Never value the entire department cost as removable.
3. Specify the desired after-state and mechanism. Explain a real example from input to useful output, with integrations and exceptions behind it. Decide whether native setup, repeatable integration or bounded custom development best solves it. The student must be able to explain and demonstrate it.
4. Compare today's alternatives: staff, software, vendor onboarding, another agency, doing nothing. Explain the actual work and result worth buying; don't require magical novelty, but reject a high fee for a trivial toggle. State what would disqualify this buyer.
5. Package the service: core deliverable, implementation sequence, client responsibilities, time to first useful result, ongoing support boundary, proof, price basis and guarantee/remedy. Bonuses must remove a real obstacle; do not inflate arbitrary values.
6. Develop risk reversal, not just 'none until later'. Propose suitable options: an outcome remedy where baseline and attribution are credible; a milestone/acceptance remedy where delivery is controllable; or a paid scoped pilot where integration uncertainty is real. Define metric, start date, conditions, remedy, fees covered and limit. 'We work free' must say which fees stop and what work continues. Mark proposed terms as proposed; do not invent a revenue guarantee or automatically ban one.
7. Check client economics and student economics separately. Client value is after review/exception work and full software costs. Student margin includes technical partner, sales, onboarding, support, revisions and capacity. Show a plausible route to $20k/month using explicit setup/retainer mix; distinguish collected setup revenue from recurring revenue. Arithmetic scenarios are not an earnings forecast.
8. Select the acquisition route to match buyer count, message, urgency and deal complexity. A concentrated account list may favour targeted calls/email; broad repeatable pain may merit ads. Do not prescribe every channel. Explain the first observable test and the result that changes the offer.

## Deliverable
First give a clear one-page offer: buyer; costly problem; promised outcome; mechanism; inclusions; time; price hypothesis or scoped fee; proposed risk reversal; evidence; next buyer/demo test. Also save a canonical offer record with every material claim tied to source, assumption or decision. Record offer version so scripts/pages/followups cannot drift.
Include two packaging options only when useful, explain which you favour and why. Reject or revise an uneconomic proposition before building its full marketing suite. A rendering request may still get a clearly isolated specimen, but it is not a recommended business.

## Understanding check
Ask the student to explain the buyer's last real problem, why the system helps, what remains human, how they would demonstrate it and how they will deliver. Coach strategy call selects the offer; technical support confirms scope. Do not make these calls an excuse to avoid doing the preparation.

## Acceptance
Can a beginner explain it in 30 seconds without jargon? Does a buyer have a reason to care now? Is the outcome more compelling than a feature list? Are both economics credible? Is the delivery path demonstrable? Are terms consistent across assets? Failure means revise, not add enthusiasm.

## Blake’s marketing preferences — confirmed 22 October 2026
For this programme’s student marketing: do not publish offer prices, deposits, retainers or dollar-valued fee waivers in ads, landing pages, VSLs or the public offer statement. Keep pricing/economics in private sales and planning documents. Lead with the buyer’s actual desired outcome (for example sold jobs and cash collected), then explain the mechanism. Intermediate metrics such as replies or appointments support diagnosis; do not automatically make them the main promise. Do not invent a revenue number to make the headline stronger.
A work-free remedy means continued agreed work without the specified management fee until the agreed result; define the target, measurement, responsibilities and costs behind the plain-language promise. Make material conditions accessible. Do not silently replace that remedy with a published price discount.

Include a separate student teaching guide: how the buyer’s business makes money, before/after workflow, buyer pains and discovery questions, what is automated versus human, alternatives, demo plan and a 30-second explanation. Keep detailed teaching out of prospect copy.
