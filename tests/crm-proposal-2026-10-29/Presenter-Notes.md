3. Explain how the work changes. Focus on ownership, next actions and fewer handovers.
4. Confirm the proposed scope. Avoid reciting a feature inventory; relate each part to a problem on page 2.

6. Confirm users, integrations, migration and the named automations. Capture exceptions before quoting.
7. Present the approved build fee, payment stages, running costs and support. Pause for the buyer's questions.
8. Review the economics using client-confirmed inputs. Keep additional revenue, contribution, released time and cash expense reductions distinct.
9. Explain the work, review points, dependencies and target dates.
