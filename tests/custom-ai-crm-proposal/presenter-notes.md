# ACT CRM proposal · live read-through

Use the page as a conversation, not a pitch script. Pause after each section and check that ACT recognises its own process.

## 1. Start with the outcome

“The goal is that a request can move from the website through quote, signature, payment and assessment with the job history and next action visible in one place.”

Ask: **“Is that the right outcome for ACT?”**

## 2. Confirm the current handoff

“You described Jotform sending details to admin, then someone re-entering them into BCS. After that, documents, proposals, signing, invoices, payment checks and inspections all have to be followed through.”

Ask: **“Which handoff causes the most repeated work today?”**

Avoid asserting a dollar loss or a missed job unless ACT supplies an example or measurement.

## 3. Walk one job through the demo

Select the six steps and explain the one record, the owner and next action. At proposal stage, point out that staff confirm the fee and choose/approve the template. At signing and invoice, distinguish a signed proposal from paid status. At inspection, keep reschedules and unclear replies visible for a person.

Ask: **“Where should a request stop for a person to review it?”**

The screen is a workflow illustration, not a live record. Say so if asked; don’t demonstrate it as connected data.

## 4. Explain what is in the build

Use the “current build” panel to show progress. Use the “before ACT go-live” panel to explain which real connections still need acceptance tests. DocuSign sandbox signing is a test; it does not mean ACT’s production sending is accepted. Existing Xero connection evidence is separate from the full invoice-to-payment handoff test.

## 5. Talk about value and price honestly

“The work this takes today is re-entry, follow-up, document handling and handover. We haven’t measured those hours yet, so I haven’t put a savings number on the page.”

Add only the agreed CRM implementation price, GST, payment milestones, ongoing service charges and support terms from the current commercial agreement. The discovery call does not contain those terms. Do not use ACT’s customer fee schedule as the CRM implementation price.

## 6. Agree the delivery path

Confirm business rules and owners first, then complete the live integrations, run labelled acceptance jobs, fix defects and get ACT sign-off before moving day-to-day work. Do not promise a date until access and acceptance scope are current.

Close with: **“If this is the right workflow, let’s confirm the first release, the remaining inputs and the agreed commercial terms, then finish the controlled team test.”**

## Decisions to capture after the call

- Approved workflow/stage names and whether/how BCS is used during transition.
- ACT owner for user roles, finance visibility, workload routing and document categories.
- Approved fee schedule, template-to-project rules and representative accepted proposal examples.
- ACT DocuSign account/signers and required owner/certifier form routing.
- Xero invoice types/tax, full-upfront default and allowed 60/40 exceptions.
- ACT mailbox, Microsoft calendar, Twilio sender and reminder/reply rules.
- Historical data/cutover boundary and objective ACT acceptance criteria.
- Actual CRM implementation fee, GST, milestones, third-party costs, support and dates.
