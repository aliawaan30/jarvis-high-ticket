# Private presenter notes — call two

The client sees the proposal, not these notes. Confirm the decision-maker is present and the goal of the call is to agree whether to proceed on the scoped solution and terms.

| Page | Your job | Natural check-in |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Proposal | State what you propose building and the outcome they asked for. Keep it short. | “Is that still the priority?” |
| 2. Problems | Remind them of the incidents, workarounds and consequences from call one. Use their language. | “Have I captured what is happening correctly?” |
| 3. Current cost | Walk through one agreed calculation. Do not inflate the model or pretend all wages disappear. | “Are those the right numbers to base the decision on?” |
| 4. Proposed build out | Connect every included part to a problem they just recognised. State the important boundary. | “Does that cover the change you want?” |
| 5. How it will work | Show one job, not a tour of every screen. Show the useful output and one human decision or exception. | “Could your team work this way?” |
| 6. Timeline | Explain the stages, review points and what you need from them. | “Can your team supply access and review at these points?” |
| 7. Costs | State the approved price, payment stages and running costs clearly, then stop talking. | Answer the actual concern. Establish whether it concerns scope, value, funding, timing or authority. |
| 8. Next steps | Ask for the decision directly. If agreed, confirm the agreement, payment and kickoff process. | “Are you comfortable proceeding with this scope and these terms?” |

Do not improvise concessions, new deliverables, deadlines or guarantees to rescue the close. Record a scope change and reprice it. Use a genuine deadline only when there is a real reason. A clear “not proceeding” or a specific unresolved decision is better than an ambiguous commitment.

The proposal supports diagnosis and the buying decision. Its design or rendering is not evidence it will close deals. Test teach-back, buyer understanding and actual calls before claiming a proven template.
