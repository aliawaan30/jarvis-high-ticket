The selection narrows the custom CRM family; it does not infer that a new CRM or AI agent is necessary. Next learning action would be problem interviews about real quote handling, not a full custom build. Interviews were not conducted in this exercise.

## Short service brief

**Buyer:** owner of an Australian residential fence installation business with roughly 2–5 staff, already using ServiceM8, sending enough homeowner quotes to make inconsistent follow-up material. Staff/volume are screening assumptions, not demand proof.

**Today and pain:** in an illustrative business, the owner measures a boundary, sends quote F-1042 for a 24 m timber fence, then goes back on site. Three days later nobody has checked whether the homeowner understood removal costs. Quotes live in ServiceM8 but chasing and deciding who owns the next action are inconsistent. We have not observed this in a real company. They would pay if a tested process saves management effort and helps viable existing estimates become profitable sold jobs; an owner already following up well may have no need.

**Change:** a sent, eligible quote triggers approved reminders; replies get human attention; accepted jobs stop quote chasing. The owner decides scope, discounts, schedule and whether further follow-up is appropriate. No autonomous quoting, negotiation or fabricated urgency. Before: remember every quote. After: routine reminders plus an explicit owner procedure for questions and stopping. Native details and sources are in evidence.md.

**Exact first scope proposed:** one existing ServiceM8 account; inspect current settings and existing automation first; design two email templates at day 3/day 7; test only controlled sample job contacts; approve a simple daily owner review routine; test no-reply, reply, acceptance, missing-contact and manual-stop cases; leave screenshots of settings, one-page operating guide, 45-minute handover and a 14-day defect correction window against the agreed acceptance tests. No migration, new CRM, SMS, AI reply generation, lead gen, old quote bulk reactivation or custom integration. Initial activation covers newly sent eligible quotes only, subject to a test proving actual platform behaviour; no global switch until impact on existing jobs is understood. If that isolation is unavailable, use a controlled manual pilot or pause scope, rather than assume it is possible.

**Client responsibilities:** provide authorised access and account owner, approve templates/timing/recipients, establish appropriate permission for follow-up, supply sample jobs and exclusions, answer customer questions, maintain job status, cancel inappropriate reminders, attend handover and own daily review. Roughly 2 hours for setup decisions/training plus ongoing reply work. Do not confuse the system with a closer or fence installation capacity.

**Delivery support:** learner handles discovery, wording, checklist and handover; technical helper verifies account configuration, isolation, stop/resume behaviour and acceptance tests before launch. Three-week delivery is a private planning estimate contingent on helper and client availability, not a proven delivery record. If helper cannot confirm safe scope/cost, do not sell this specification. No implication the learner is a ServiceM8 Partner.

## Why pay and an explicit illustrative calculation

All volume, labour, margin and conversion figures below are assumptions, not vendor results or buyer numbers. AUD cash model, with tax treatment to be confirmed commercially.

- 40 quotes/month × 2 manual checks × 4 minutes = 320 minutes, or 5.33 hours of routine follow-up.
- New daily review and exception handling assumed at 2 hours/month. Capacity released = 3.33 hours. At an illustrative A$45/hour, that is **A$150/month of time value**, not a wage reduction or collected cash. If review takes longer there may be no time saving.
- Separately, assume one genuinely additional completed and paid fence job each month. A$4,800 revenue less A$3,600 additional materials/labour/other variable costs = **A$1,200 contribution**. No counting A$4,800 as profit; no extra conversion assumed on all quotes; no adding both revenue and margin.
- With a hypothetical A$99/month support charge and no incremental licence cost for an existing suitable account, extra cash contribution is **A$1,101/month** in that one-job scenario. It is **minus A$99/month** if there are zero extra jobs and no cash labour saving. We do not add the A$150 time value to this cash total or count software replacement savings.
- A hypothetical A$900 setup plus A$99/month costs A$2,088 in year one. At A$1,200/job, cash break-even is 1.74, therefore **two additional completed/paid jobs over the year**, provided no other incremental costs. The scenario of 12 extra jobs/year would give A$14,400 contribution less A$2,088 = A$12,312; this is sensitivity arithmetic, not a forecast.
- Native Starter is advertised at A$29/month including GST, with 50 new jobs and 100 SMS. It is already paid in this target case; email-only scope adds no quoted SMS charge. A new or upgrading buyer has different costs/fit and is outside this first scope. Verify account quota and total job count rather than assuming 40 quotes means 40 total jobs.

Questions before using this in a proposal: how many open quotes, which receive no follow-up, why do they lose, what is actual variable contribution, can they install extra work, what time is affected, and will the owner use the process? Compare pre/post sold jobs and collected contribution over a meaningful period; seasonality and pricing confound attribution.

## Strongest alternative and implementation value

The strongest alternative is **enable ServiceM8's own Quote Follow Up and use its DIY guides**. It already provides the core function; there is no credible claim our software is unique. [Native documentation](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/servicem8-add-ons/automation/quote-follow-up-automation) and [vendor setup options](https://www.servicem8.com/au/) are inspected evidence.

Someone might pay for inspecting the existing workflow, choosing appropriate wording, checking exceptions in their account and teaching the team to use it. That is a delivery hypothesis. A technically comfortable owner with clean records should DIY; an accredited existing partner may be a stronger implementer than this novice. Discovery must establish that the implementation friction warrants the fee. This small service does not by itself substantiate a high-ticket positioning or A$20k/month path. Proprietary AI would add cost without established benefit here.

## Private student economics and pricing hypotheses

A$900 setup and optional A$99/month are unvalidated testing hypotheses, not public offer prices or price acceptance. No retainer should be sold without a defined ongoing job: proposed monthly 30-minute settings/status review, issue log and one minor template revision. Exclude customer reply handling and new workflows.

First delivery plan: 3 hours discovery/admin, 3 hours learning/settings preparation, 2 hours templates, 3 hours assisted setup/tests, 2 hours guide/handover, 2 hours contingency = **15 learner hours**. At 10 hours/week plus acquisition time, allow three weeks with one client; avoid overlapping first builds. Assign A$35/hour opportunity cost = A$525, not actual wages paid.

Private cash allowance: helper 3 hours × assumed A$90 = A$270; demo/tool allowance A$30; cash contingency A$100 = **A$400**. Quotes for helper/time are not obtained. A$900 less A$400 = A$500 cash contribution before taxes, acquisition and owner labour. After A$525 learner time value, **minus A$25** on this first project. Another 5 acquisition hours valued at A$35 makes it minus A$200. The service may be a learning-sized entry project; a beginner must not confuse positive cash with attractive profit.

Monthly hypothesis: A$99 less A$17.50 learner time, A$22.50 helper escalation reserve (0.25 hours × A$90) and A$10 tool allowance = A$49 before tax/acquisition/extra incidents. Reserve is a planning allowance, not evidence of actual frequency. Client pays its existing licence directly. No course fee included because profile does not specify whether already paid; if A$1,500 must include tuition, affordability is unknown. A$400 first-build exposure leaves A$1,100 liquidity; no purchased leads, speculative software stack or ad spend. Costs could exceed this, so obtain a helper quote and contract scope before a paid deal.

## Six-line output and demo

`six-line-offer.md` is exactly the required six labelled lines using the same brief. It is an offer hypothesis, with the risk-reversal field explicitly to confirm; no invented refund/guarantee. The delivery timeframe is proposed, not a promise of extra sales in three weeks.

`demo.html` is a standalone offline walkthrough: F-1042 → choose a sample event → see reminder preview or human action. Main sequence is no reply → reminder; realistic exception is old-fence removal question → owner. Five scenarios are provided. Fictional sample data is visible. No external requests, forms, real addresses, credentials or sending code. It models decisions; it does not implement timers, mailbox ingestion, ServiceM8 updates, automatic opt-out detection or real message delivery.

The **business result** sought is incremental profitable sold work and cash collected. The **demo capability** is displaying one understandable decision path. Seeing the reminder preview cannot verify the business result, actual account operation or native UI. Staff-led manual-stop procedure is deliberate; answering/dismissing a reply can resume native automation.

“They might get more profitable work from estimates they already issued, but I can't promise an extra job. ## Friction log and lesson implications

| Point in proposed journey | Simulated friction | What was needed / suggested lesson fix |
|---|---|---|
| Optional examples | Catalogue headline says scaling and contains confident arbitrage/profit implications. A novice could treat counts as proof despite skill caveats. | Skill caveats helped; put the evidence distinction beside examples, and show one adaptation without inheriting advertiser promises. |
| Buyer/problem choice | No niche means many plausible ideas, with no actual buying evidence. The service brief stopped me choosing solely on demo ease. | A short worked example of selecting one reachable buyer and listing interview questions; no pass/fail score pretending to prove demand. |
| Native alternative | Native software made the AI agency idea feel less special. Without checking it, I might sell a custom CRM needlessly. | Explicit comparison: DIY, paid setup, custom build. Make native-fit research a named step before six lines, with a stop condition if fee/value is weak. |
| Service brief → six lines | Brief makes the offer explainable; however skill mandates a done-for-you money-back remedy and the handoff forbids inventing guarantee terms. | Retain “to confirm” for a draft. Teach a system acceptance promise and a later commercially approved remedy separately; novice cannot approve a guarantee from research. |
| Delivery promise | Three weeks sounds concrete but no account or helper quote exists. A demo isn't a scope estimate. | Show how access, sample tests, client duties, helper quote and acceptance criteria turn a draft into a supportable commitment. |
| Economics | A$900 appears attractive until 15 student hours and acquisition are counted. Native licence is already included. | Worked cash versus time versus contribution example, including zero-extra-job sensitivity and capacity. Do not teach wages or software stack totals as cash savings. |
| Small demo | Course's general “build demo” instruction doesn't tell a beginner what to omit. | The handoff's one-input/exception/output is sufficient. Give a minimal fixture recipe and a checklist for checking actual logic separately from visual polish and revenue proof. |
| Technical handoff | “Technical support available” still leaves rates, hours, permissions and accountability unknown. | Sample pre-sale technical feasibility request, and what happens if existing-job isolation fails. Group calls cannot be the only route to this knowledge. |

Strongest issue: checking native software changes the commercial proposition from “AI system” to paid implementation. A beginner needs permission and a worked method to reject weak customisation economics, rather than make the offer sound more impressive. The handoff repairs the understanding gap; the actual six-line skill still leaves an unresolved guarantee field.
