# Beginner offer run A · final selection after one failed branch

## Plain-English decision brief

Candidate offer: help Australian landscape builders already using Buildxact stop losing approved site extras between the crew and the next invoice. The buyer gets one mapped variation process, agreed owner/client approval steps, a bookkeeper reconciliation routine, three tested sample cases and staff handover. They keep their software; no custom CRM or AI pricing is needed.

They might pay if recent jobs show recurring completed extras that never reach a paid invoice, and fixing the handoff is worth more than doing it themselves. This is a research hypothesis, not validated demand. The first quote-reminder idea was rejected because native software already handled it and beginner delivery economics were negative.

Decision: carry this stronger variation-handling idea into discovery and a bounded technical quote. Do not sell yet. Confirm actual lost charges, native-account fit, helper cost and team adoption; reject it too if the evidence does not justify implementation. The offline demo explains one request-to-approval-to-invoice sequence. It proves no cash recovery.

**AI simulation of a beginner, not a human usability test.** Australia; no agency/coding experience; 10 hours/week; A$1,500 available; comfortable talking to owners; bounded technical support available. Status: internally tested offline concept, unvalidated commercial hypothesis. No actual buyer, outreach, account connection, payment, deployment or Base44 test.

## Short service brief

**Who buys:** owner of an Australian residential landscape construction business with perhaps 3–8 staff, multiple fixed-price garden/paving projects and Buildxact already adopted. Size, software use and recurring lost extras are hypotheses to confirm, not exclusion rules or proof of willingness to buy.

**Today:** fictional job 104 includes paving. On site the homeowner requests another 12 m². Crew mention it by phone; owner prices it later; client says yes; bookkeeper may not see the same scope or whether the extra reached the next invoice. The assumed pain is unpaid or disputed work, repeated chasing and uncertainty about which version was agreed. No real workflow has been observed.

**Why pay:** protect the collectable value of authorised completed extras and reduce handoff errors. This matters only if the owner can show recurring leakage or costly rework, has appropriate contract processes and will change team habits. A buyer with a working native process should not buy this service.

**Before/after:** verbal request and fragmented handoff → crew records change against job → owner verifies scope/cost/timing → client reviews exact version → owner authorises work under their process → bookkeeper checks approved, completed and unbilled variations. Exceptions: missing specification, declined/changed scope, already or partly billed. No AI sets prices, infers permission or authorises work. This is an operating procedure using native software, not a new app.

**Exact first scope:** one existing Buildxact account, one fixed-price job type and one crew; inspect a redacted sample of 10 recent variation handoffs; establish roles and three required fields (scope, owner price, approval record); configure up to three reusable descriptions/cost-category conventions using supported settings; write a mobile entry/checklist procedure; set an owner release rule and bookkeeper reconciliation routine; test three core cases (complete/approved, incomplete/revised, already/partly billed); provide one-page guide, two 45-minute staff sessions and 14-day defect correction against agreed tests. No CRM replacement, custom integration, automatic payment, accounting migration, contract drafting or cost-plus job automation. Templates/checklists can be written procedures where the platform lacks an appropriate field; no invented native configuration capability.

**Client does:** authorised access; existing subscription; owner-approved job/contract variation requirements, tax handling and rates; redacted samples; nominate owner, crew and bookkeeper; attend sessions; verify every scope/price; collect appropriate approvals; release work and issue/check invoices; handle disputes and collect payments. Estimated 3 hours setup participation plus ongoing operating work. Do not sell legal compliance or legal enforceability from this workflow.

**Learner/helper:** learner maps the process, drafts plain guides, coordinates tests and trains; technical helper verifies account settings/permissions and actual approval-to-billing behaviour. Four-week delivery is proposed after access/decisions; helper cost/availability not quoted. If required controls cannot be supported, use an agreed manual control or decline the scope. No production work was performed here.

## Native alternative and meaningful fee test

**Buildxact's own mobile variations plus approval and invoicing workflow is the strongest alternative.** It already supports the underlying records, so a separate AI/custom system is unjustified. The paid value hypothesis is mapping crew/owner/bookkeeper handoffs, making scope/version checks routine, reconciling approved extras to invoices and proving the team can do it. DIY or an experienced vendor partner may be better. No partner status is claimed.

A meaningful implementation fee is plausible only where confirmed leakage and avoided effort exceed the change cost. This simulation does not establish that. Go/no-go discovery: review recent records with the owner; identify repeated real omissions, reasons and amounts; distinguish customer refusal from bookkeeping omission; confirm ongoing volume, owner accountability and helper quote. If there is no material unresolved gap, reject this offer too. Do not raise the price just to fit the course's high-ticket theme.

## Illustrative benefit calculation without double counting

All operational figures are invented assumptions. AUD, before GST/tax, no financial projection offered to a buyer.

Assume 10 variations/month, A$1,800 average selling amount each, A$1,200 incremental delivery costs, and one in ten currently completed but omitted from invoicing. Assume the process recovers **one such genuine omitted charge/month** and it is collected. Its incremental collection can be A$1,800 if delivery costs were already incurred; **we conservatively use only A$600 contribution** (A$1,800 minus A$1,200) in the value case. We do not also count the A$1,200 cost, A$1,800 revenue, fewer disputes or admin time as separate savings. These are not new sold jobs; the mechanism targets cash from extras on existing projects.

Hypothetical A$2,500 setup, no mandatory retainer and no incremental licence for the target existing suitable account: year-one fee A$2,500. At conservative A$600 per recovered variation, break-even is 4.17, therefore **five additional collected variations over a year**. Twelve would imply A$7,200 contribution less A$2,500 = A$4,700. Zero would mean minus A$2,500. At just two recoveries/year the case loses A$1,300. No claim all 120 annual variations are lost, collectable or affected. Discounts, nonpayment and additional administration reduce benefit.

Private time illustration: 10 variation handoffs × 15 minutes = 2.5 hours/month before; after assume the same total time spent documenting properly. **Zero time-saving credit** in the case. The workflow might increase admin effort initially. Benefit requires more than a neat screen.

[Buildxact AU pricing](https://www.buildxact.com/au/pricing/) shows Pro A$399/month monthly or A$339/month billed annually (A$4,070), excluding GST. The text extraction does not preserve feature checkmarks; exact variation/invoicing entitlement must be confirmed, not inferred. A buyer newly adding A$399/month has A$4,788 extra annual licence cost, changing year-one total to A$7,288; even 12 × A$600 = A$7,200 would fail that conservative case. This is why first scope targets a suitable existing account, and switching software is not a hidden recommendation. No software replacement savings counted.

## Private delivery costs and pricing hypotheses

A$2,500 implementation is **unvalidated willingness-to-pay**, not a public price or established market rate. Assumed student hours: 4 discovery/process, 4 platform learning, 3 configuration/procedures, 4 assisted tests, 3 training/handover, 4 contingency = **22 hours**; 4 weeks at 10 hours/week leaves some acquisition/admin capacity. Add 6 acquisition hours for a total of 28.

Assume helper 5 hours × A$100 = A$500; tools/demo allowance A$50; cash contingency A$200 = **A$750 cash exposure**, leaving A$750 of the learner's available funds. Helper quote not obtained; do not spend against an unbounded build. Existing laptop/internet and already-funded course access assumed. Tuition is excluded; if budget must cover that too, affordability is unknown.

A$2,500 fee minus A$750 cash = A$1,750 before owner time/tax. Student time valued at A$35 × 28 = A$980 leaves **A$770 economic contribution** before tax/insurance/other overhead. At 10 more unplanned hours and A$300 extra helper cost this falls to A$120. This is a testable first project, not proof of scalable income. No recurring price assumed; a later support scope needs its own cost/fee review. The 14-day correction allowance must fit contingency or be re-estimated before sale.

## Offer, demo and evidence boundary

`six-line-offer.md` contains exactly six labelled lines following the actual creation skill, based on this brief. No public price, invented authority or guarantee. Risk reversal is to confirm before sale because the skill explicitly allows an unknown draft field; its compulsory remedy wording is not authority to fabricate terms.

`demo.html`: job 104 / V-008 / add 12 m² paving → owner review → approval/billing handoff. Sample states include missing specification, client pending, accepted/unbilled, changed scope and fully billed. Numbers, approvals and invoice reference are fictional, visibly labelled. It demonstrates the logic of a procedure; it does not generate genuine signature evidence, update Buildxact, verify a contract, issue an invoice or collect cash. The business outcome is collectable completed extra work reaching paid invoices; the demo only shows what the next action could be.
