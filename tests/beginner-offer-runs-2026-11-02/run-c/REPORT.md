**Decision:** carry supplier-price preparation forward as a comprehensible discovery hypothesis and demo, **reject selling it as a justified high-ticket custom build today**. Native imports and Proceny already cover most of the workflow. A bespoke project is justified only if an actual buyer's file/rule/adoption requirements exceed these alternatives and its workload supports the economics. ## Plain-English brief

A purchasing manager at a UK specialist wholesaler receives supplier price sheets, matches product codes, checks pack sizes and prepares an update for Unleashed. We would configure a workflow that does the repeatable preparation, holds uncertain lines, and produces a checked file after the manager approves it. The client keeps its existing stock system and runs the import.

A business might pay a specialist to map its own data, adapt difficult files, test the import and train its staff. But Proceny plus Unleashed already offers much of this. There is no demonstrated reason yet for a bespoke high-ticket build. Under the private illustrative model, roughly 4,000 eligible lines every month at 24 seconds net saved per line merely covers first-year spend in staff capacity value; around 8,000 reaches an assumed 2× comparison. Neither means cash savings.

**Verdict:** understandable, bounded discovery hypothesis; reject a high-ticket recommendation until a buyer demonstrates sufficient recurring work, an incumbent gap and a feasible technical quote.

## One-page service brief

**Buyer:** purchasing/operations manager, with owner/finance approval, at a UK specialist B2B distributor already using Unleashed. Indicative 10–50 staff is a prospect-screening assumption, not proof of budget. The situation matters more: several suppliers, thousands of eligible price lines, inconsistent files and recurring manual preparation. We have no count of UK firms meeting those filters.

**Current process and pain:** someone receives supplier spreadsheets, identifies which supplier item is which internal product, checks price basis and pack quantities, edits the existing import template and asks a colleague to approve it. Changed codes or pack sizes trigger questions. The work repeats; stale or wrong supplier purchase prices can misinform purchasing and margin decisions. E1/E2 support the category of problem, not this precise buyer scenario or its cost. The following sample is fictional: NORTH sends N-002 at £18 per pack of 12, while the existing record is £16 per pack of 10. Treating this as an ordinary price increase without noticing the changed pack would mislead the operator.

**Why fix it:** remove repeated copying and shorten the queue to an accurate approved cost update. Human capacity may be redirected to purchasing work; reducing stale costs might protect margin only if the buyer demonstrates pricing decisions affected and can act on them. Updating a cost does not itself increase customer prices, recover cash or create sales. Do not sell saved wages unless overtime, outsourcing or headcount expense actually changes.

**What is supplied:** a bounded supplier-file preparation workflow around the existing stock system, product cross-reference table, exception review, approved template generation, operator training, reconciliation checklist and handover. First examine Proceny/native imports; configuration or Excel may be sufficient. Custom adapters/rules are conditional on demonstrated gaps. No reason to rebuild inventory software or make this an unrestricted AI agent.

**Evidence/alternative:** see [evidence.md](evidence.md), E1–E6. Strongest DIY stack is Proceny catalogue matching/normalisation/export plus Unleashed's own importer. Gauge is an established overlapping specialist offer. The remaining work a buyer *might* pay for is preparing their cross-reference data, adapting an unsupported supplier layout, preserving their exact import template, proving exception behaviour, training staff and owning the handover. No inspected evidence proves these products fail at that buyer's requirements. If the native/product route works easily, recommend it and reject the custom build. A small configuration job must not be relabelled high-ticket because the course wants one.

## Exact system and delivery boundary

- **Inputs:** client's actual Unleashed Product Suppliers template and current catalogue snapshot; five agreed CSV/XLSX supplier layouts; supplier identity, GBP net prices, stated effective date, supplier product code and price basis/pack. Maximum 10,000 lines/month across these layouts for first scope. This is a proposed boundary, not a tested capacity.
- **Actions:** validate layout and required fields; exact supplier+code mapping against approved links; compare existing price/unit; calculate price change; flag duplicates; stage changes by effective date; produce review list tied to original row. Where AI assists later with varied documents, extracted values remain suggestions; first scope needs no LLM.
- **Exceptions:** unknown/ambiguous code, blank or nonpositive price, currency/unit mismatch, changed pack, inconsistent basis, duplicate file/row, obsolete product, stale catalogue, future/missing effective date and unusually large changes. Hold them. Missing list rows never delete catalogue products. Purchasing manager owns clarification and mapping; developer owns parsing failures.
- **Approval/output:** purchasing manager checks eligible changes and source/effective date. Generate only approved changes inside the client's actual preserved template, plus held-items list and audit record. Authorised client operator backs up, imports and reconciles approved versus resulting values. Import failure stays unresolved; reruns require duplicate/stale checks. No autonomous ERP writes.
- **First scope:** five known spreadsheet layouts, one Unleashed organisation, GBP, one purchasing approval role, one verified supplier-specific price template, one documented handover and bounded correction period. Exclude scans/PDF/OCR, multi-currency, supplier negotiations, customer sell prices, landed-cost revaluation, inventory/stock movement and API writes. Two to three historic files/layout are needed to test layout drift.
- **Delivery support:** paid technical reviewer first checks templates and native/product fit, then quotes adapters/review UI, preservation/duplicate controls, import test/rollback plan, access and data handling. Client provides authorised sample data, current export, mapping owner, acceptance cases and staff time. Student leads discovery, records scope, coordinates tests and trains alongside specialist; cannot claim to implement unsupervised.

**Acceptance proposed, not agreed:** approved supplier-specific fields match source for an agreed mixed test set; untouched template fields preserved; all agreed exceptions held; reprocessing same file cannot create a second change batch; approval invalidates when input changes; operator can reconcile actual import and recover original values in test environment. Real expected match rates, throughput and time saving require a measured baseline and pilot, not invented targets.

## Private illustrative economics — not a quote or public price

All inputs below are assumptions for learning, not buyer data, market rates or quoted contractor costs. Net minutes already subtract continuing review/exception work; do not count those twice.

| Scenario | Eligible lines/month | Net minutes saved/line | Capacity hours/month | At assumed £28 loaded/hour | Annual capacity value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | 2,000 | 0.30 | 10 | £280 | £3,360 |
| Base | 6,000 | 0.40 | 40 | £1,120 | £13,440 |
| Upside | 9,600 | 0.50 | 80 | £2,240 | £26,880 |

Formula: eligible lines × net minutes ÷ 60 × loaded hourly cost. Monthly workload is not established; lists may be quarterly. No margin-recovery value included, no new sales assumed and no payroll saving claimed. Cash saving could be zero in all three cases. Any verified avoided overtime or credit must be modelled separately without double-counting labour or assuming all errors become recoverable margin.

For a *stress test only*, assume £6,500 implementation plus £200/month support inclusive of £50/month tool/hosting allowance: first-year incremental spend £8,900 excluding VAT, plus client onboarding time. Existing Unleashed licence remains in place and is neither replaced nor counted as savings; unknown incremental licence costs need adding. Assume 8–12 client hours onboarding/testing (another £224–£336 at £28 if that is the right employee cost), plus normal residual review already deducted in net minutes. First-year capacity-value/spend ratios before onboarding are 0.38× / 1.51× / 3.02×. These are capacity comparisons, not cash ROI. The conservative case fails even before implementation risk. No sale price is defensible from these inputs.

To cover £8,900/year in *capacity value* at £28/hour needs 26.49 net hours/month. At 0.40 min/line, that is about 3,974 eligible lines/month. That merely breaks even under these assumed costs; it is not an approved qualification threshold or buyer willingness to pay. At 2× benefit/cost, an explicitly chosen comparison, about 7,947 such lines/month would be needed before onboarding costs. Quarterly work must use annual volume rather than multiplying a busy month by twelve.

Illustrative delivery: developer 45–65 hours × £50/hour = £2,250–£3,250; 25% uncertainty reserve gives £2,812.50–£4,062.50. At the illustrative implementation fee, gross remainder is £2,437.50–£3,687.50 before student labour, sales, tax and liability. Student 24–36 hours across six weeks (4–6 hours/week) leaves 6–8 of their 12 weekly hours for discovery, learning and administration; only one pilot at once. Assumed monthly developer support 1.5 hours × £50 + £50 tools = £125, leaving £75 of a £200 support fee before student account work and unexpected incidents. These are fragile allowances, not a scoped support plan.

**Funding gate:** £2,000 cannot safely prefund the assumed build. Allocate at most an illustrative £300–£500 to technical/native-fit scoping, keeping £1,500+ uncommitted; obtain a real quote and payment schedule before any sale. Deposits are not evidence of delivery economics. Do not contract a guaranteed result or spend the remaining budget on speculative development. If no viable staged arrangement exists, this learner cannot currently fund the custom delivery.

## Two-minute learner explanation (simulated teach-back, not a recorded human test)

“I am looking at specialist wholesalers in the UK that already use Unleashed. They buy from several suppliers, and new price sheets arrive in different layouts. Someone has to line up supplier codes with the products in their stock system, check whether the price is each or per box, and prepare the import. That can take repeated staff time and leave the system using old costs. We have evidence that distributors deal with this, but I do not yet know how much it costs a particular UK firm.

The service prepares that update for the person who owns purchasing. Here is a fictional file. The first product has a straightforward price change. This second one changed from a box of ten to a box of twelve, and the last code does not match anything. The system holds those two rather than guessing. I check the source and approve the one eligible line. Only then does the review pack appear. In a real installation the authorised operator imports the verified template and checks the result.

I would coordinate discovery and training, with a technical specialist building any needed adapters and testing the import. The client still decides ambiguous products and purchasing rules. We would begin with five known file layouts and one organisation, not replace the inventory system.

The point is less repetitive preparation and more current, checked purchasing information. Saved time is capacity, not automatically money in the bank. Proceny and the native importer may already do enough. Before recommending a custom build I need to see why those do not work, measure the recurring workload, and get a technical quote. Right now I have a demo and a hypothesis, not a proven high-ticket offer.”

## Course and skill friction observed

1. **Critical bridge still missing in actual lesson files:** current `m2-write-the-offer.json` says Jarvis already has research and asks “[which one from the list]”; current menu lesson ends “Pick one.” Deep custom route and readable service brief are supplied by the handoff/skills, not taught reliably in the actual lesson. Without that external bridge C could name an “AI pricing agent” without understanding pack/unit or purchasing ownership.
2. **Research method mismatch:** main custom skill asks for one short card while its reference asks broad requests to aim for ten implementations, market counts, cost models and acquisition. Parent explicitly bounded this run to three options. A novice would need a clear stopping rule: one workflow, strong alternative check, sufficiently understood scope, and labelled missing buyer data. More volume of research would not establish a product gap.
3. **False readiness risk:** lesson market score “20 or more means go”, staff-count budget signals and owner mobile availability cannot settle a purchase decision. No numeric score was used here; the strongest alternative defeated automatic readiness.
4. **Value arithmetic risk:** current irresistible lesson combines CRM/admin costs and uses missed calls × close rate × job value. C instead separated capacity from cash and withheld margin/revenue value. Line volume, residual work, cadence and actual avoided cost require direct observation; sales experience alone does not supply these.
5. **Marketing promise pressure:** current offer lesson says “They do nothing, they risk nothing” and suggests a mechanism name makes it sound proprietary. This implementation needs client data, decisions and adoption; suppliers and native products already use the mechanism. Skill output correctly marks unique status unestablished and proposed remedy incomplete. Its exactly-six-lines rule is useful for packaging but cannot replace service understanding.
6. **Guarantee conflict:** research-method says guarantee optional until supported; offer skill requires a remedy line, yet says not to invent terms. Draft line therefore says proposed money-back remedy with conditions to confirm. A beginner needs a supported “to confirm” state, not pressure to invent a refund period or revenue guarantee. No approved terms or public price here.
7. **Demo expectations:** current `m2-build-the-demo.json` universally says build before talking and “Jarvis builds it in a day.” An offline three-row demo took no integration work; production needs real templates, permissions, parsing cases, approval/audit storage and import reconciliation. Demonstration proves a visible sequence, not installability, savings or demand. The handoff's smallest-demo instruction was essential.
8. **Support ambiguity:** “technical help available” does not establish a quote, available developer hours, support response or who owns production incidents. C cannot independently settle exact template preservation, safe API writes, licence tier, secure access, provider suitability, contract or funding schedule. The optional support must have an accessible pre-sale feasibility route; the later scoping agent cannot be treated as a pre-sale quote.
9. **Budget/time feasibility:** researched custom scope could exceed £2,000. A polished six-line card would hide that failure unless private economics are required upstream. This test produced a defensible pause at discovery, not a license to presell any build.
