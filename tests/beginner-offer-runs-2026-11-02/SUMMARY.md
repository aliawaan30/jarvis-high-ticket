# Three simulated beginner offer runs

2 November 2026. These are AI agents simulating beginners, not human student tests. They used the proposed teaching journey and actual canonical discovery/offer skills. This was not an end-to-end Base44 integration test or a test of every published lesson. No outreach or sales occurred. Three routes tested: optional catalogue adaptation, outside idea, deeper custom research. No new live Meta Ad Library sweep.

## What came out

### A. Get paid for approved landscaping extras

Buyer: Australian residential landscape builders already using Buildxact.

Problem: the homeowner asks for extra paving, the crew does the work, but scope, approval and invoice handoffs get separated. A completed extra can be omitted or disputed.

Service: map one crew-to-owner-to-client-to-bookkeeper process so each change is recorded, priced by the owner, approved and checked against the next invoice. Keep existing software. This is an implementation/training service, not proprietary AI.

Plain offer direction: **Help landscape builders get completed, approved extra work onto their invoices instead of losing it between the site and the office.** A four-week implementation is proposed, not established delivery performance; a commercial remedy remains unapproved.

Illustrative economics: A$2,500 implementation; valuing each genuinely recovered/collected extra at A$600 contribution, five such recoveries in a year exceed that fee. Neither frequency nor buyer willingness is established. Native Buildxact already supports variations and billing, so the owner must need implementation/adoption help worth paying for.

Verdict: intelligible entry project, weaker fit as the flagship high-ticket AI service. Do not disguise native setup as a novel AI build.

### B. Stop retyping emailed purchase orders

Buyer: US industrial-supply distributors with substantial repeat emailed orders.

Problem: staff read purchase orders and copy customer details, product codes and quantities into another system. Ambiguous pack sizes and duplicate orders need checking.

Service: read three agreed customer order layouts into a draft; check approved product/customer codes; hold unclear quantities and duplicates; let staff approve a CSV draft for the existing order system. Pricing, stock, credit and order release stay with the business.

Plain offer direction: **Turn repeat emailed orders into checked drafts, so your team can process them with less retyping.** Proposed four-week pilot with a bounded acceptance-test refund hypothesis; not a revenue promise or an approved contract.

Illustrative economics: 1,200 orders/month, seven minutes today versus review/exception/oversight work totalling 52 hours/month would free 88 hours. All inputs are assumptions. At a hypothetical US$3,500 setup, genuine avoidable overtime could support a cash case; released staff capacity alone is not cash savings. Tools, review time, target import and specialist quote can change the conclusion.

Verdict: strongest of these three for further discovery and sample testing. Real provider deployments support the problem category; transferring the economics to a smaller buyer and proving a paid implementation gap remain open.

### C. Check supplier price changes before updating the stock system

Buyer: UK specialist wholesalers using Unleashed.

Problem: someone matches supplier price sheets to internal products, checks pack sizes and prepares an update. Wrong units or changed product codes can corrupt the update.

Service: match agreed supplier files, hold unusual changes, collect purchasing approval and prepare the correct import file. No autonomous inventory writes or pricing decisions.

Plain offer direction: **Prepare checked supplier-cost updates without manually rebuilding each price sheet.** Proposed six-week pilot; no measured savings deadline.

Illustrative economics: 2,000 eligible lines/month at 0.3 minutes net saved only produces ten hours of capacity, worth £280 at an assumed £28/hour. That does not support the illustrative £6,500 setup plus £200/month ongoing cost. More volume and a real unsolved requirement are necessary.

Verdict: reject the high-ticket recommendation for now. Proceny plus native Unleashed imports overlaps strongly. A neat demo does not establish a worthwhile custom service.

## What the test exposed

Two runs initially picked weak native-software setup offers: ServiceM8 quote reminders and OrangeQC cleaning inspections. The supervising agent challenged their value and alternatives, leading to pivots. Keep that intervention visible: these were not unaided beginner successes.

The service brief made the system understandable. The commercial selection still needs an explicit review before recommending an offer:

1. Is the problem material at the buyer's actual volume?
2. Why would they pay us instead of using native software, vendor onboarding or staff?
3. Is AI/custom development necessary, or are we selling ordinary setup?
4. Can the student explain and arrange the work within their time, budget and support?
5. Does the proposed promise follow from controllable delivery and measured inputs?

Commercial value includes useful capacity and service quality; the test should not reject a good service solely because it cannot remove a salary. But it must not invent recovered revenue or assume all saved time becomes money.

The raw six-line cards are useful internal drafts but overly caveated and long for public copy. Improve that separation: keep evidence and unknowns in the brief, then write a concise public message using approved facts and terms. No canonical skill was rewritten in this test.
