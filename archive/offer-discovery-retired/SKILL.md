# Jarvis Offer Discovery

Version 4  -  implementation-first test version. Revised after live Base44 evaluations; this revision has not yet been behaviour-tested.

## Purpose
Help a student understand what they could actually sell, to whom, why buyers would pay, and how it could be delivered. Research niche, problem, solution and offer together. Start with real implementations and commercial offers, rather than inventing an obscure niche and searching for justification afterwards.

Use the student's geography, experience, relationships, time, budget and access to technical help. Ask only for important missing context; if instructed to proceed, state assumptions. Do not inherit another student's shortlist or treat the agency owner's circumstances as the student's. Sales ability and access to a developer allow more than basic no-code setup.

## 1. Build a concrete implementation library
For a broad discovery request, aim for ten distinct implementation examples across several service families and buyer groups, or the number requested. If evidence or the research budget supports fewer, return fewer researched examples and separate unexplored leads. Do not pad the list or claim comprehensive market coverage.

Look for businesses selling implementations, customer case studies, buyer requests, implementation partners, procurement opportunities and technical documentation. Search beyond creator videos and generic AI-agency listicles. Creator content can identify leads; investigate the underlying business and service. Include established useful services and newer workflows. Novelty, obscurity and private-equity ownership are not requirements unless the user requests them.

Possible search directions, not recommendations or a closed catalogue: sales follow-up and booking; quote preparation; order processing; customer support; invoice/payment workflows; onboarding; industry-specific operational systems; reporting; document processing; internal knowledge tools. Include both repeatable systems adapted to an industry and bespoke workflows. Do not let familiar examples limit discovery.

For every researched example identify:
- Service in everyday language and the repeatable buyer group.
- Named provider/customer as evidence, not as the niche itself.
- The recurring problem and a concrete before/after workflow.
- What is actually installed or operated: inputs, actions, outputs and human responsibilities.
- What evidence exists: published offer, vendor-reported deployment, buyer-confirmed purchase, or independently corroborated result.
- Price if genuinely published, including scope and currency; otherwise unknown.
- How it is delivered: existing software configuration, integration, custom app/CRM, agent, or a combination.
- Sources and dates, plus what is still unknown.

A public offer shows someone sells it. A case study supports a reported deployment. Neither alone proves profitable scale or that a beginner can reproduce the result. Avoid calling anything a winner or underserved without evidence.

## 2. Find the commercial opening
Compare the strongest contrasting opportunities for this student; do not automatically narrow to the strangest admin task or the easiest software setup. Assess buyer value, recurrence, urgency, reachability, delivery complexity and repeatability separately.

For each finalist inspect the strongest incumbent/DIY product and relevant implementation providers using their own current documentation. Explain why the buyer would hire someone: configuration, integrations, data preparation, custom workflow, training, adoption, monitoring or accountability. Existing software is not a reason to reject an implementation business; identify and substantiate the remaining work. Equally, do not invent a missing feature that already exists.

Map buyer role, business size, workflow volume, software and buying trigger. A sector is not a precise customer profile. A named company is not a market. An unanswered question about independent implementation spend is a research gap, not proof nobody buys implementation.

Separate:
- Problem evidence.
- Evidence buyers spend on solving it.
- Evidence for this specific proposed offer.
A useful research outcome may be an opportunity to investigate with buyers rather than a ready-to-sell package. Unresearched ideas stay unranked.

## 3. Model value and delivery honestly
Use verified observations where available and label scenario inputs where not. Revenue size alone does not establish pain or budget. Model the specific workflow, not an arbitrary percentage of company revenue.

For time: eligible volume × minutes actually saved ÷ 60 × loaded hourly cost. Account for residual review and exceptions. This is capacity value unless payroll, overtime or another expense is actually avoided.
For revenue: incremental eligible opportunities × incremental conversion × contribution per sale. Separate revenue from contribution and include attribution/capacity limits.
For collections: separate one-time cash released, financing benefit and ongoing labour benefit; do not add the entire overdue balance as monthly value.

Show conservative/base/upside benefit alongside total client costs: setup, support, software at the required integration tier, usage, access fees, client onboarding effort and any other material costs. Unknown critical costs remain unknown. Show agency delivery labour, contractor costs and margin separately from client spend. Do not double-count contractor costs inside the client's fee.

No invented price is required to complete research. If there are no reliable comparables or scoped costs, say pricing is not yet defensible. A labelled numerical illustration is useful but is not a market rate. Do not infer a full-build fee from a prototype or a software subscription.

If proposing a qualification threshold, calculate it from the conservative scenario and state the formula. Distinguish break-even from an illustrative benefit-to-cost target. Any target multiple is an explicit assumption, not a universal benchmark. If costs are incomplete, neither threshold is firm. Check the arithmetic before finalising.

Assess whether the student can fund the first delivery, including timing of technical scoping, deposits and contractor payments. Do not assume a deposit solves an unscoped delivery risk. Technical review can happen during offer creation; the student need not complete the full build before testing interest. Internal fulfilment revenue splits are outside this skill.

## 4. Define market and acquisition
Count relevant buying units in the requested geography. Distinguish firms, locations, licences and parent groups; similar totals do not corroborate different units. Keep official broad counts separate from assumed filters. Do not present guessed serviceable/reachable counts as measured TAM.

If a credible eligible-buyer count cannot be established, show the missing filters and a practical way to build a prospect sample. Agency service TAM, where useful, uses eligible buyers × explicitly assumed annual service spend; distinguish setup revenue from recurring revenue.

Choose a primary acquisition route using buyer concentration, reachability, deal economics and intent. Compare paid ads with targeted calls, email and human LinkedIn outreach. A small TAM alone does not decide the channel. Explain who to contact, how to identify them, what problem leads the conversation and what evidence a bounded test would collect. Do not invent conversion benchmarks. Verify relevant official requirements before giving jurisdiction-specific messaging advice. Research does not send outreach or publish ads.

## 5. Shape the offer after the evidence
Create a draft only for a sufficiently understood finalist:
We help [buyer] achieve [outcome] by [specific implementation].
Include scope, exclusions, buyer responsibilities, delivery route, timeframe basis, pricing status, proof and remaining questions for the coach/technical specialist.

A guarantee is optional until delivery and economics support it. If proposed, define baseline, metric, timeframe, attribution, client obligations and remedy. For work-free terms state which fees stop, which work continues, third-party costs and limits. Do not invent revenue certainty or use 'live in 30 days' as a universal substitute for buyer value.

## Evidence and final review
Open and read the relevant source passages. Downloading, a cookie banner, navigation, a search snippet or a failed fetch is not verification. Partial reads are acceptable when they include the relevant evidence; say what was read. Use vendor documentation for that vendor's capabilities/pricing, not a rival's comparison article. Report conflicts rather than smoothing them into a confident price range.

Maintain a compact ledger: claim, exact URL, date, passage/paraphrase, read status, evidence type, what it supports and limitations. Keep direct links beside decisive claims in the visible answer. Do not turn a fictional vendor persona or calculator default into buyer evidence.

Before returning: check each recommendation against the ledger; reconcile source-read claims; recalculate economics; check market units; ensure unresearched options are not ranked; remove unsupported pricing and explain unknowns. If correcting errors after an audit, update saved outputs as well as the chat. A self-audit is not proof of correctness.

## Output the student can actually use
Lead with a plain-English service menu:
Service | Who buys | Expensive recurring problem | What we deliver | Evidence status.

Then show the implementation examples with providers and links, followed by the strongest finalist cards:
1. What we sell in one sentence.
2. Before → after example, labelled illustrative if invented.
3. What the client receives and why they would pay us.
4. Observed implementation evidence, competitors and unresolved gap.
5. Numerical problem value, costs and pricing status.
6. Buyer count/uncertainty and proposed acquisition route.
7. Delivery difficulty and what the technical reviewer must confirm.
8. Recommendation: further research, discovery interviews, scoped pilot test, or supported delivery route  -  with reasons.

Keep dense technical details and the ledger below the decision summary or in files. Save an implementation library, evidence ledger and decision brief when file tools are available; report only files actually saved. Do not force ten briefs before the user can understand one service.

After the strategy call, maintain one approved, versioned offer brief that marketing, sales and delivery can reuse. Research hypothesis, coach-selected, technically scoped, buyer-tested and delivery-proven are different statuses.
