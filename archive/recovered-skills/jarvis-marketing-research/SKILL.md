# Jarvis Marketing Research

Build the evidence and creative direction behind a student's acquisition. Start from their current offer and connect every recommendation to an identifiable buyer and business problem. Produce research and briefs, not a generic list of AI trends.

## Start from the shared business brief

Read the latest supplied or connected offer brief: target buyer/geography, recurring problem, service/mechanism, scope, price, timeline, guarantee terms, proof, delivery route and version/approval status. Also use known acquisition results and sales objections where provided.

Niche, problem, solution and offer are interdependent. If research undermines one, propose a change to the whole combination and explain the tradeoff. Do not silently rewrite an approved offer. If the student has no offer yet, research a small set of complete opportunities and prepare a recommendation for their strategy call rather than inventing a final approved positioning.

The programme workflow is: student completes research/course preparation → brings options to Blake's strategy call → coach refines the offer → technical CSM checks scope, costs, feasibility and delivery route → approved brief feeds acquisition. Marketing research does not replace that decision.

Clarify missing geography or buyer only when it materially changes research; otherwise state a reasonable research assumption. Record the date and search period. Use current sources when asked what is happening now. Without browsing, label the output a research plan or analysis of supplied material, not fresh market research.

## Research the buying situation

Investigate the recurring workflow, who suffers the problem, who owns the budget, what triggers action and what happens if nothing changes. Look for concrete evidence:

- Buyer interviews, supplied sales recordings, reviews and customer questions.
- Existing spending, hiring for the task, implementation case studies and current workarounds.
- Competing offers, service demonstrations, pricing, guarantees, scope and onboarding.
- Barriers to change: access, migration, training, reliability, internal approval and prior disappointments.

Prioritise primary sources for what a provider sells. A vendor case study is still vendor-reported performance; a buyer interview or account data may provide stronger evidence for that buyer. Label evidence accordingly. Competitor activity shows supply and positioning; it does not alone establish buyer demand, profitable scaling or an underserved niche.

Evaluate advanced repeatable workflows, CRM implementations, custom operations apps and automations when relevant. Do not default every student to websites or receptionists, and do not discard those routes if they fit the actual offer.

## Inspect competitors and funnels

For each useful example, record buyer, promise, mechanism, deliverables, price if public, timeframe, guarantee, proof shown and next-step CTA. Examine the journey available to you: ad → landing page/VSL → application or booking → thank-you/nurture. Mark inaccessible steps as unknown. Do not claim to have watched a VSL if only its page or title was accessible.

Study why the structure could work: message continuity, qualification, explanation, demonstration, objection handling and effort required to act. Distinguish observed structure from your interpretation. A polished funnel, long-running ad or popular video is not verified conversion performance. Preserve reference links; do not copy a competitor's distinctive wording, fabricate their results or present their client proof as the student's.

For video research capture relevant timestamps when available and identify whether you used footage, transcript, description or title only. Creator education and a provider's actual client offer are different evidence types; label both accurately.

## Diagnose awareness and differentiation

Use Eugene Schwartz's awareness framework as a practical lens, not a fixed label for an entire industry:

- Unaware: does not yet recognise the problem in this form.
- Problem-aware: recognises the problem but needs a way forward.
- Solution-aware: knows solution categories and needs to understand fit.
- Product-aware: knows the offer and is comparing proof, scope and tradeoffs.
- Most-aware: understands the offer and needs a clear next step or resolved final concern.

Assign an awareness hypothesis per audience/traffic source, with the observed questions or behaviour supporting it. Separate this from market sophistication: what competing promises and mechanisms buyers have already encountered. Technical AI literacy is a different variable.

Identify the desired outcome and the credible mechanism connecting the service to it. Use Hormozi's value lens to examine expected outcome, believable success, time and customer effort; this is a design heuristic, not a predictive equation. From buyer interviews, look for concrete purchase triggers, attraction to alternatives, anxieties and habits. Do not invent psychographic personas to fill gaps.

## Build testable angle briefs

Create a focused set of meaningfully different angles based on the evidence, not dozens of synonyms. Each brief contains:

- Buyer segment and buying situation.
- Problem/desire and awareness hypothesis.
- Core angle and proposed hook direction.
- Body logic: problem/context → mechanism → relevant proof/demo → actual next step, adapted to the audience.
- Available proof and visual demonstration; unsupported claims to exclude.
- Relevant objection or implementation anxiety.
- Offer version and CTA destination.
- Why this angle is worth testing and what result would help assess it.

Keep hook directions and example phrasing clearly labelled as drafts. Blake's approved ad scripting and VSL references are still required to establish the final house style. This skill does not claim to encode those unwritten skills or produce a finished VSL by default.

Plan tests that distinguish angles from cosmetic variations. State what changes and what remains comparable. Follow the campaign strategy and measurement definitions actually supplied; do not invent universal spend thresholds or scaling rules. Judge creative in context of qualified leads, bookings, attendance, closes and acquisition economics when available, not click-through rate alone. Separate attribution, date range and sample size from interpretation.

## Return reusable evidence

Lead with a short decision summary: strongest finding, recommended angle/opportunity and most important uncertainty. Then provide:

1. **Research scope:** date, geography, sources/method and coverage limits.
2. **Evidence ledger:** claim or observation, source URL/artifact, publication date if known, access date, evidence type and confidence rationale. Short exact quotes remain exact; paraphrases are labelled.
3. **Buyer and competitor picture:** recurring problems, current alternatives, buying triggers and meaningful gaps supported by evidence.
4. **Messaging diagnosis:** awareness by segment, competing claims, mechanism and proof needed.
5. **Angle briefs:** actionable inputs for the downstream ad, VSL and funnel work.
6. **Validation and handoff:** buyer questions to resolve uncertainty, changes for the strategy/technical call, and which approved brief version downstream work should use.

Use an explicit not-public/unknown value for private pricing, missing metrics or unobserved funnel steps. Do not infer revenue from ad spend, followers, views or an unverified screenshot. Keep collected cash, booked revenue and profit distinct. New results, scarcity or guarantee terms require evidence/approval; they must not emerge casually during copy generation.

Save research and briefs to the designated workspace/CRM when available and requested. Otherwise return structured findings in chat. Do not contact buyers, subscribe to services, launch ads or change campaign spend as part of research alone.
