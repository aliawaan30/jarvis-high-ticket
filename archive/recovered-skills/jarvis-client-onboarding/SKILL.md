# Jarvis Client Onboarding

Create the shortest useful path from the client's agreement to a delivery-ready project. Derive every request from their specific service and existing context.

## Establish what was agreed

Read the signed scope or accepted proposal, relevant sales notes and the client's current records. Identify the agreed deliverables, price/payment milestones, timeframe, dependencies, decision-maker, promised result and support terms. Reference the source and date. A sales-call suggestion is not automatically a contractual deliverable.

When sources conflict, flag the exact decision for the student to resolve. Do not silently broaden the scope. If only a draft proposal exists, produce a draft onboarding pack and label it accordingly; do not announce the client as onboarded or the agreement as signed.

## Prepare questions by delivery need

Reuse known answers and ask only what is missing. Group the remaining questions by the action they unblock:
- **Process:** How does the workflow operate today? Who handles exceptions? What must remain manual?
- **Data:** Which real sample records or documents demonstrate normal and difficult cases? What needs migrating, and what is the source of truth?
- **Systems:** Which exact products/accounts are involved? Who administers them? Which integrations and plan levels are available?
- **Users:** Who uses the system, who can see/change which records, and who approves delivery?
- **Outcome:** What is the current baseline, where will results be measured, and who confirms acceptance?

Select only relevant questions. A quoting automation may need sample enquiries, pricing rules and approval thresholds; a CRM may need pipeline definitions, roles and migration mapping. Do not send both clients the same generic technical questionnaire.

## Build an access checklist

For each required system, specify purpose, required role/permission, client admin responsible, connection method and status. Prefer an account invitation or the platform's authorization flow over asking for passwords or secret keys in chat. Record whether access is requested, granted, tested or blocked; possession of a login alone is not proof the required integration works.

Connect or configure tools only when the student has requested that action and the relevant tools are available. Do not imply this skill automatically creates integrations or sends onboarding messages. When sharing is authorized, send only to the intended recipient and report what was actually sent. Otherwise provide ready-to-send copy.

## Turn scope into delivery milestones

Create a small plan appropriate to the service: inputs/access ready -> build or configuration -> internal tests -> client acceptance -> go-live -> handover/support. For each milestone record owner, dependency, due date or proposed duration, and completion evidence. Leave dates proposed until agreed. Client delay may change the plan; flag impact rather than changing commercial terms yourself.

Derive acceptance criteria from the scope. If absent, propose criteria for the student and client to agree. Include normal cases and important exceptions. Keep business outcome measurement alongside functional tests rather than treating a passing demo as proof of revenue results.

Identify the few blockers that truly prevent starting and the work that can proceed independently. Route unresolved technical feasibility to the technical CSM or delivery partner with the relevant evidence. If a new request appears, record it as a scope-change request with impact to cost/time; do not quietly promise it.

## Return the pack

Lead in chat with the three immediate actions (normally under 300 words), and save the full pack separately when possible. When scoping has already produced a summary, reuse it and add only onboarding-specific material; do not repeat the entire technical brief.

Provide:
1. **Client summary:** agreed result and scope source, key contacts, current status.
2. **Client checklist:** concise questions and requested materials, grouped by priority.
3. **Access table:** system, purpose, required role, owner, status.
4. **Delivery plan:** milestone, owner, dependency, target, acceptance evidence.
5. **Open decisions:** conflicting commitments, missing information and scope changes.
6. **Welcome message draft:** explain next steps and who is responsible, using the agreed offer and timeline.

Keep internal cost estimates and unresolved commercial discussions out of the client-facing message. Include only known commitments. Update existing project records instead of creating duplicate clients or tasks when tools permit. Without project tools, provide a copyable plan and say it has not been synced.

At handover, check client acceptance, ownership/access, operating instructions, support boundaries and the outcome-measurement schedule. Capture recurring client confusion so the student can improve their offer explanation and future onboarding.
