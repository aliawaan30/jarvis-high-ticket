# Fit and evidence

Use an explainable rule set based on the offer. Do not assign a universal numerical pain score or treat public symptoms as proof of an expensive problem.

Record four separate assessments:

1. Account fit: geography, business activity, delivery eligibility and exclusions.
2. Workflow relevance: observable reason to investigate the problem this offer solves.
3. Buyer identity: what supports the role hypothesis.
4. Reachability: available eligible contact routes.

Tier A: matches the account definition, has a credible offer-specific reason to investigate and a practical contact route. Tier B: plausible fit with a material question. Tier C: weak fit or a documented exclusion. Unknown information is not automatically C. Record the reason alongside the tier.

For every signal save the observation, source/date, interpretation and a discovery question. Example: a recent vacancy asks someone to maintain several spreadsheets. The observation is that job requirement; the hypothesis is repeated manual reporting; the question asks how the current process works. Do not claim a salary saving or wasted hours from the vacancy alone.

No booking link on an inspected page does not prove lost bookings. Listed hours do not prove nobody answers after hours. Old visual design does not prove slow systems. Site technology does not establish the internal CRM. Checking a form's actual destination would require an interaction outside this pilot.

Member checks the first ten, records mistakes and revises the rule version. Review all 30 pilot accounts and sample lower-ranked accounts to find false negatives. A/B/C rankings are proposed priorities; no claim that ranking increases replies until a suitable later experiment measures it.
