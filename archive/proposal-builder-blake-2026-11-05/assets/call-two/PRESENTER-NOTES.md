# Private presenter notes: call two

The client sees the short proposal, not these notes. Confirm the decision-maker is present and that the call is to decide on the scoped work and terms.

1. **Overview:** name the proposed build and priority. “Is this still the result you want?”
2. **Problem and impact:** use their actual incidents and agreed numbers. “Have I captured this correctly?” Do not invent a savings model while presenting.
3. **Proposed build out:** connect each part to its problem. “Would this solve the issue we discussed?” State what remains with staff.
4. **Separate demo, if useful:** open its own file/tab, show one workflow and exception, then return to the document. “Could your team work this way?” Do not turn it into another proposal section.
5. **Timeline:** confirm access, reviews, testing and handover. “Can your team provide these inputs?”
6. **Costs and decision:** explain approved price, ongoing costs and payment stages, then ask directly whether they want to proceed. Address the actual concern: scope, value, timing, authority or funding.

Record new scope and reprice it; do not improvise concessions, promises or guarantees. Agree a specific next step when the decision is pending. A clear no is not a signed deal. Judge buyer understanding and actual calls separately from document rendering.
