# Private discovery inputs

Complete before preparing the client proposal. These questions and sourcing notes do not go in the shared-screen document.

1. What happened the last time this problem caused trouble? Record the buyer's actual example and words.
2. Who does the work now? Walk through each step, system, handover and exception.
3. How often does it happen? Get a count for a defined period, not “quite a lot”.
4. How much time does each distinct task take, and what does that person's time cost? Avoid overlapping task estimates.
5. What cash cost, delay or lost contribution results? Separate wages already paid from overtime, a hire avoided, and genuine revenue loss.
6. What must remain with staff? What outcome would make this worth doing, and how would it be measured?
7. What existing software already helps? Establish the specific gap and confirm access/integration feasibility with the technical person.
8. Who approves spending, who will use it, and who else needs to attend call two?
9. What is included: users, records, workflows, integrations, migration, reports, approval rules and support?
10. Confirm the scoped delivery estimate and approve the fee, running costs, payment terms and any guarantee before producing an issue-ready quote.

For every important number, save its source, period, unit and whether the buyer confirmed it. A cost model is not evidence by itself. If economics, access or scope are unknown, return to discovery or propose a bounded paid scope instead of pretending an implementation quote is ready.
