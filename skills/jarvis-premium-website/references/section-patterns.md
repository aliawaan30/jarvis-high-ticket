# Section patterns

The building blocks for a site plan. A site is composed from these, chosen by the research (what buyers need to see) and styled by the brand guideline. Use each pattern only when the research gives it a job. Most sites use 8 to 12 sections.

Each pattern lists: its job, when to use it, and the phone arrangement.

## Section order: follow the buyer's questions

Order the sections so each one answers the next question the buyer has, roughly in this order and adapted to the niche. This is planning logic, not a visible style: do not number sections on the page unless the chosen style calls for it (for example the editorial monograph archetype).

1. What do you do, for whom, and what should I do next? (the hero)
2. Why should I act now? (the problem and the cost of doing nothing, made concrete)
3. What would it be like? (the story scene: one realistic slice of time in the customer's life)
4. What exactly do I get? (the offer, often as a diagram of how the pieces fit)
5. Does it really work? (proof: a labelled concept demo, real reviews, real work)
6. Is it for me, and do you cover my area? (audience and service area)
7. How does it work, what does it cost, and how do I start? (process, price or estimator, FAQ, booking)

Skip any question the research shows this buyer does not ask.

## Heroes (pick one)

1. **Scroll film hero** (Cinematic tier). Job: create the feeling in one shot. Pinned film controlled by native scroll, headline and main button usable before the film loads. Phone: still frame, subject composed for portrait, headline below the image.
2. **Full-bleed still hero.** Job: a strong first impression without motion. One art-directed photo, headline on a text-safe area. Phone: crop for the subject, headline below if it covers the subject.
3. **Split hero.** Job: show the offer and the proof at once. Image on one side, headline and action on the other. Phone: image first, then text.
4. **Editorial hero.** Job: a quiet, confident statement. Large type on a plain field, small detail image. Phone: type first.
5. **Proof hero.** Job: trades and emergencies. Headline with two key numbers or facts and a call button. Phone: sticky call bar from the first screen.

## Trust (use what the research says buyers check)

6. **Trust strip.** Certifications, licence, review score with count, years. Confirmed facts only.
7. **Providers or team.** Real people, real roles and licences. Required for health and medical aesthetics.
8. **Reviews.** Real reviews with name, date and source, and the owner's replies where they exist. Never written by us.
9. **Before and after gallery.** Only consented, unaltered, dated pairs; where rules require, with a typical-results note. Never generated.
10. **Credentials and partners.** Real badges and memberships, linked to the issuer where possible.
11. **Case study.** One real project: the problem, what was done, the outcome in the client's numbers.

## Offer and explanation

12. **Intro statement.** Two-column: a short headline and the positioning paragraph.
13. **Numbers band.** Three or four confirmed figures that matter to the buyer. Counts up once, never loops.
14. **Services grid.** Three to six services, each with an image, one line and its own action.
15. **Service detail rows.** Alternating image and text, for services that need explaining (treatments, packages).
16. **Pricing.** "From" prices or packages, when the client agrees and the market expects it.
17. **Process steps.** Three to five numbered steps. Reduces fear of the unknown.
18. **Feature spotlight.** One differentiator explained with an image or film and three points.
19. **Comparison.** Us versus the usual way, factual and fair, never naming competitors.
20. **Second film chapter** (Cinematic tier). A mid-page film, separated from the hero by real reading content.

## Local and practical

21. **Service areas.** Neighbourhoods or suburbs, from the client's confirmed list.
22. **Location and hours.** Map link, parking, hours, accessibility.
23. **First visit or what to expect.** For appointments: arrival, consultation, aftercare.
24. **FAQ.** The real questions from the research's search layer. Expandable, keyboard friendly.

## Action

25. **Quote or enquiry form.** Few fields, the real destination, a clear note on what happens next.
26. **Booking embed or link.** The client's real booking system.
27. **Offer band.** A new-customer offer, only if the client runs one, with its terms.
28. **Closing statement.** A large final line and the main action.
29. **Sticky phone action bar.** Call and book or quote buttons fixed at the bottom on phones.

## Footer

30. **Footer.** Business details, licence numbers where required, privacy page, terms, social links, the main action again.

## Editorial and storytelling (from the Delta Cafes study, 18 November 2026)

31. **Chapter hero.** Two or three full-screen film or image chapters that advance on their own, with numbered chapter buttons and a progress line; pauses on hover; still for reduced motion. Phone: chapters stack as a short list under one looping hero.
32. **Endless row.** Products, portraits or logos sliding slowly in a loop; pauses on hover and focus; stops for reduced motion. Real items only.
33. **Heritage timeline.** Three to six real milestones with real archive photos. For young businesses: "how we started" in three real moments.
34. **Tabbed journey.** A process (source, roast, brew; consult, design, plant) as tabs, each with its own image and two lines.
35. **Profile finder quiz.** Three to five questions that recommend a product, service or style, then lead to the main action. Real recommendations only.
36. **Journal teaser.** The three latest posts with dates; only if the client will actually publish.
37. **Full-screen image menu.** A menu overlay where each link shows its own image on hover or focus.
38. **Ambient sound toggle.** Optional room tone, always off by default, never autoplaying, clearly labelled.

## Story, diagrams and tools (only when their trigger is true)

Add each of these only when its trigger is true for this client; otherwise leave it out. A site with none of them can still be excellent.

- **Story scene:** the value happens over time (an evening of calls, a lunch-break visit, a busy weekend).
- **Animated diagram:** something needs explaining (a process, who does what, what is included, an area covered).
- **Built before and after:** a visible change is what the client sells, shown with real work or clearly labelled concept mockups.
- **Estimator:** the price depends on the customer's choices (areas, units, sessions, size).
- **Scroll progress bar:** long pages only.

39. **Story scene.** One realistic slice of time in the customer's life ("one evening", "one Monday morning", "one lunch break"), pinned on scroll, with timestamps, a timeline and a live simulation built in code (an inbox, a booking list, a dashboard, a message thread) filling in step by step. Works at every media level. Phone: it fits one screen, keeps its timeline and its simulation, with headroom for the header and the action bar.
40. **Offer diagram.** What's included, drawn as a diagram of how the pieces fit, with the brand mark as the central node.
41. **Built before and after.** A deliberately dated "before" against a premium "after", both built as real markup (not screenshots), both labelled Concept.
42. **Estimator.** A simple calculator when the price depends on choices (area, units, sessions), showing a range with [placeholders] until the client confirms prices, and leading to booking.
43. **Scroll progress bar.** A thin line at the top showing reading progress, in the accent colour.

## Property and product recipes (from a live build, 27 November 2026)

- **Property or residence intro:** a short introduction, a full-bleed image reveal, a row of key specs, then the materials.
- **Materials:** macro photographs of each material (stone, wood, metal, fabric), with a pinned scroll that crossfades between them, plus where each comes from and how it is finished.
- **Gallery:** a clean editorial grid with varied image sizes and short captions, opening in a lightbox with arrow keys; nothing decorative.
- **Listing or offer details:** the price prominent, the facts in a two-column list with short labels, generous space, nothing dense or boxed in.

## Choosing patterns

- Start from the brief's site plan: each planned section maps to one pattern.
- Vary rhythm: alternate full-width and contained, image-led and text-led, light and dark.
- Never put two heavy media sections next to each other.
- The action appears at least three times on a long page: hero, middle, close.
