# Image direction

Good images placed as equal cards still look like a catalogue. Decide how every image is used before generating anything, because the composition decides the shape, and the shape decides the shot.

Learned from the Fernhollow Gardens test (18 November 2026): the images were strong, but almost every one sat in an even grid of small 4:3 cards, there was no large image moment after the hero, every image was the same shape, and empty placeholder boxes took up space.

## 0. Media must prove the offer (decide this first)

Learned from an AI agency site (19 November 2026): cinematic nature films looked good but had nothing to do with selling AI receptionists and websites. Beauty is not enough; every visual must help sell.

1. **The relevance test.** Every image, film or animation must show at least one of: the buyer, the buyer's problem, the product working, or the result. If it shows none, cut it, however beautiful.
2. **Choose the media type from what the site sells:**

| The site sells | Lead with | Generated media is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A place or an experience (gardens, restaurants, real estate, med spas, hospitality) | Cinematic photos and films of the place, the work and the result | The place, details and atmosphere (never fake people or fake results) |
| A tech, software or outcome-based service (AI receptionists, websites, automation, agencies, apps, B2B services) | **The product working:** live demos animated in code (for example a missed call, the AI answering, the conversation, then a booking landing in the calendar), real screenshots in phone and laptop frames, short screen recordings, a "listen to a demo call" audio player, before and after examples | The buyer's world only: the problem moment (a roofer on a roof as his phone rings unanswered, a full waiting room, an empty diary), shot so no real person is implied as a customer. Never abstract beauty shots or unrelated scenery |
| A physical product | The product itself, in use and in detail | Product scenes and textures |

3. **Code-built demos beat generated video for software.** Interfaces, notifications, chats, calendars, dashboards and phone screens should be built in code from the brand's tokens: crisp at any size, editable, accurate, and they never look AI-generated. Use generated video for the real world, never for screens.
4. **Real demos are labelled.** A demo call recording made with the member's own AI receptionist, a demo website or a sample dashboard is fine, labelled "Demo" or "Sample", with no invented client names or results.
5. **Never generate screens or devices.** A generated monitor, laptop, tablet or phone cannot show a real interface: it comes out blank or full of fake text. Learned 23 November 2026, when an AI agency site got two blank monitors as its "website" image. Screens are always code-built interfaces or real screenshots placed inside a device frame drawn in code.
6. **Nothing off-palette.** Every line, border, bar, frame and highlight uses the brand's colour tokens. A stray default colour (for example a bright blue progress bar) is a defect.
7. **Hero choice:** for tech and outcome services, the hero is usually a live product demo beside the headline, not a film. For places and experiences, the hero is usually the film.

## 0a. Choose the media level (first decision)

Decide this before anything else in the media plan, and write it with a one-line reason at check-in 1.

| Level | Uses | Suits | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Opus only** (the default) | Code-built visuals from code-visuals.md plus the client's real photos | Outcome and expertise businesses: AI and tech services, agencies, legal, accounting, consultants, coaches, B2B, and trades with good real photos | Nothing |
| **2. A few photos** | Level 1 plus three to six Higgsfield stills where real photos are missing and photography matters | Trades without real photos, med spas, dentists, clinics, local services | A few dollars |
| **3. Cinematic** | Level 2 plus Higgsfield films and a transformation scroll where they earn their place | Businesses whose work or place is what people buy: gardens and landscaping, restaurants and cafes, hotels and venues, real estate, interior design, events | Within the media budget |

The four visual approaches map onto these levels: **Cinematic** is level 3; **Photo-led** is level 2 (or level 1 with the client's own photos); **Diagram-led editorial** and **Typography-led minimal** are level 1. Recommend one approach after the research, with a one-line reason, and let the member choose.

The deciding question: would a customer choose this business because of how its work or place looks? Yes means level 3; partly means level 2; no (they buy an outcome) means level 1. If the client has strong real photos, use them and drop a level. The member can always ask for a higher or lower level.

## 0b. Does the hero need a video? (decide, never default)

A hero video earns its place only when the movement shows something a still cannot: a place's atmosphere (mist, water, light), a process happening, or a transformation. If a still says the same thing, use the still. Videos cost money, slow the page and often look generic when they do not earn it.

| The business | Best hero, usually |
| --- | --- |
| Places and experiences (gardens, restaurants, hotels, real estate) | A video: atmosphere is the product |
| Tech and outcome services (AI receptionists, software, agencies) | A code-built live product demo, not a video |
| Trades (roofing, plumbing, HVAC, electrical) | A strong still or a real job photo, with the phone number prominent; save motion for the transformation scroll |
| Professional and medical (legal, accounting, dental, med spa) | Editorial typography with a still; video can feel salesy |
| Any client with strong real photos | Their real photo |

Overrides: a type-led brand can make the headline itself the hero; with a tight media budget, spend it on the one signature moment that matters most (usually the transformation scroll) rather than the hero. Write the decision and its reason into the media plan at check-in 1, so the member can ask for a video if they want one.

## 1. Give every image a role before generating

| Role | Shape | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | 16:9 film or still, subject clear of the headline area | The first screen |
| Chapter break | Full width, 21:9 or 16:9, one line of text on it | Between major sections, at least twice per page |
| Split | Tall 4:5 or 3:4, bleeds to the screen edge | Beside a text column, edge to edge |
| Sticky | 4:5, several of the same framing | Stays on screen while steps scroll past and changes per step |
| Feature | Arch-topped or shaped frame that fits the brand | One signature image per section |
| Detail | Square or 4:5 close-up | Overlaps a larger image, adds depth |
| Texture | Macro surface (soil, leaves, stone, fabric, grain) | Very faint background behind a dark or plain section |
| Gallery | Mixed shapes in a masonry or offset grid | Real portfolio only |

Write the role map in the site plan: every section, its image role, the desktop shape, the phone shape (see mobile-design.md), and the planned shot. Then generate each shot in the shape its role needs, with the subject placed for that crop and a text-safe area where text will sit.

## 2. Composition rules for every page

1. At least two full-width image moments after the hero (chapter breaks, or a full-width section background).
2. At most one even grid of equal cards on the whole page.
3. Mix at least three shapes on the page: tall, wide, square or close-up.
4. At least two sections where images interact: text over an image, an image bleeding to the screen edge, overlapping images, a sticky image that changes, or an image that changes with tabs or hover.
5. One signature frame shape that belongs to the brand, used at most twice, never on every image (for example arch tops for a garden company, soft rounded corners for a med spa, sharp full-bleed for a contractor).
6. Faces and products are never cropped awkwardly: set the focal point for each crop.
7. Empty content sections (portfolio, reviews, team) are hidden until the client supplies real content. Never show rows of empty boxes on a page the client will see.
8. **Every image fits its section's meaning.** "Where we work" shows places (streets, a garden from the path, the neighbourhood), not soil. "Our team" shows people, never objects. Choose by meaning first, beauty second.
9. **The variety check.** In the site plan, list each section's layout and image treatment. No two sections share the same combination (for example, "large framed image left, list right" may appear once). No two neighbouring sections put the image on the same side. The signature frame appears at most twice. If two sections clash, change one to a different pattern from this file or the section library.
10. **Follow the niche.** Use the patterns the niche design profile says fit; never the ones it says to avoid (for example no arch frames or moody textures for real estate).

## 3. Image patterns (add to the section plan)

1. **Full-bleed chapter break:** a full-width image or short film, 60 to 80 percent of the screen height, with one line of text in a text-safe area.
2. **Edge-to-edge split:** text on one side, a tall image filling the other half to the edge of the screen.
3. **Sticky image, scrolling steps:** a large image stays on screen while steps scroll beside it; the image changes as each step reaches the middle.
4. **Hover or focus preview list:** a list of services or areas; a large image beside it switches to match the item pointed at or focused.
5. **Tab image swap:** one large image crossfades when tabs change (seasons, packages, treatments).
6. **Overlapping pair:** a large image with a smaller detail image overlapping one corner, offset.
7. **Shaped frame:** the brand's signature frame (arch, soft corner, circle) on one feature image per section.
8. **Texture background:** a macro texture at very low opacity behind a section, under the text colour, with contrast still checked.
9. **Image as section background:** a full section with a darkened or colour-tinted image behind the text, contrast checked.
10. **Panorama band:** a very wide image strip, used for places and service areas.
11. **Offset gallery:** real work in mixed shapes, staggered, not an even grid.
12. **Scroll reveal:** images rise gently or unmask as they enter the screen, once, and stay still for reduced motion.

## 4. Motion for images

- Reveal once: a gentle scale from about 1.04 to 1, or an unmask, over 0.8 to 1.2 seconds.
- Optional slow drift on large images while scrolling, a few percent at most.
- Crossfades of about 0.6 seconds for swaps.
- With reduced motion: no drift, no scale, instant swaps.
- Lazy-load everything below the first screen; give every image its real width and height so nothing jumps.
