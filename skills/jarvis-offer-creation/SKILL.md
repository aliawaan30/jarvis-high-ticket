# Create the six-line offer

Read the selected opportunity, actual delivery capability, evidence and member preferences. Reuse the choice; do not restart research. If a critical fact is absent, ask one concise question before the card, or mark the field “to confirm” when asked for a draft.

## Before you write
1. Use what you remember as a starting point, not a fact. If the member already has an offer, ask: "Is this a new offer, or a change to your [niche] offer?" A member can work on more than one offer. For a new offer, confirm in one line what still applies (city, background, service) and what is different.
2. Never assume a city, location, number or fact the member did not state. Ask. Any number you choose yourself (turnaround, thresholds, tool costs, targets) is a proposal: mark it "proposed" or ask.
3. Ask the member, in one message, the two things only they can decide: which guarantee they are comfortable with (for example work free until the target, money back on the setup fee, free revisions, first one free, or none), and how many hours a week they can give. Also ask which size of business they will give the guarantee to, for example "$100k+ a month in revenue". Put it in the niche line and the risk reversal, so only buyers they know they can get the result for are offered it. Build the risk reversal from their answer. Never swap in a different type.
4. After they answer, send only a short summary (niche, service, guarantee type, hours) and "Ready for me to write your offer?", then stop and wait. Never put the offer in the same message as a question. Write it only after they say yes, or when they ask for it directly.

Return the offer in exactly this format, each label bold on its own line, one sentence per line (two short sentences at most for the risk reversal), then stop:

Your offer (draft, needs your approval)

**Niche:** [specific buyer and situation]
**Outcome:** [the business result they want, such as extra paid jobs, with a proposed number when the research supports one; not software installation or setup completion]
**Timeframe:** [setup period, an early win (for example the first booked job within 7 days of going live), and the result period; supported or explicitly proposed]
**Risk reversal (proposed):** [the remedy and target, who it applies to (the size qualifier), what counts toward the target, and what the client must do; "work free" covers the member's own fee only, never the client's ad spend or tool costs; approved terms or a clearly proposed remedy for review; delivery model does not determine whether it is money-back or work-free]
**Authority / proof:** [the member's actual relevant evidence or an honest demonstration/experience statement]
**Unique mechanism:** [the concrete way the solution produces the outcome in everyday words, and how little the client has to do, for example "we set it up and run it, you just show up to the booked jobs"]

Approve it, or tell me what to change.

Never add a seventh line, a summary or a one sentence version unless asked. Keep "(proposed)" on the risk reversal, and on any target the member has not explicitly approved, until they approve the exact wording. When they approve, save it to Agency HQ's Offer table as the active version (approved by member: yes, with the date) and say "Offer approved and saved."

Use the member's approved risk-reversal terms; a delivery-model label does not choose the remedy. State the result and material remedy conditions within that line, preserving proposed versus approved terms. Do not invent a guarantee, refund amount, deadline or unlimited-work commitment. Competitor results are not the member's proof. Keep unknowns unknown.

No public pricing, dollar-valued fee waivers, bonus stack, appendix, teaching guide, funnel copy or extra summary. Economics, technical scoping and contract drafting belong in their respective workflows.

## Working rules
1. Talk like a builder, not an engineer. No test counts, route names, file paths, colour codes or code in your messages. Technical detail only if asked or if the member must do something.
2. Read, don't recall. When asked about records, read them and quote the actual rows. Use saved dates for "when". Only list open items that exist in the saved record.

## Context before the six lines

Reuse a saved working brief with the buyer, current problem, consequence, proposed workflow, human involvement, delivery route and evidence. The wider coordinator develops that brief before invoking this skill; do not turn the six-line response into a research essay. Distinguish setup timing from result measurement. Two to five working days may fit a simple receptionist setup after access is ready, but is not a promise for all integrations. Demo proof must be identified as a demonstration, not past customer results. A named mechanism needs a concrete explanation; giving ordinary software a name is not proof of exclusivity.

## Outcome-first review

For revenue-growth services such as receptionists or follow-up, the outcome is the additional business result: jobs, sales or customers. Answering calls and installing software describe the mechanism and delivery, not the desired result. The timeframe measures the promised result, separate from setup time. Use only approved targets or mark draft terms as proposed.

Check teaching examples and landing-page translations against the same six-line brief. Do not write a setup guarantee as the flagship example for an outcome-led service. Blake’s approved teaching wording is: “We’ll get your plumbing business 5 extra jobs in 30 days with an AI receptionist that answers your missed calls, or we work for free until we do.” This is a teaching offer, not evidence of customer results or a universal guarantee for members. Match the promise to the actual service and buyer; do not force a sales target onto a service whose agreed value is something else.

## Make it hard to say no

Check the draft against the four things that make an offer valuable, then fix the weakest one before showing it:
1. Outcome: a result the buyer wants, with a number where one is supported.
2. Belief: will this buyer believe it works for them? If the member has little or no proof, strengthen belief another way, chosen with the member: a live demo the buyer can try, a short trial period, or a small paid first step. Mark it proposed until the member agrees. Never invent proof. Proof must exist today: a demo that is not made yet is a next step, not proof. If the member has any real result, use it as the proof, with where and when it happened. Never write "no results yet" when they told you one.
3. Speed: show an early win, not only the final result.
4. Effort: say what is done for the client and what little they must do. Less work for the client is a reason to buy.

A performance guarantee must say what counts toward the target and what the client must do, for example "jobs booked by the receptionist from calls your team missed; you give call access and attend the jobs". No fake scarcity, countdowns or bonus stacks.

Before showing the offer, check the lines agree: the same target and the same time window in the outcome, timeframe and risk reversal, no line contradicting another, and the guarantee only in the risk reversal line. Use the local currency of the member's market, for example AUD in Australia.

If problem value is missing and the member asks for pre-call preparation, use jarvis-offer-menu's pre-call value research method first. Keep that sourced estimate in the saved brief. Then return the six lines from this skill; do not bury research in the offer card or pretend scenario values are confirmed client results.
