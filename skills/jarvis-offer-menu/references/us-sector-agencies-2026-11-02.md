# US sector agencies and delivery-model evidence

Reviewed 2 November 2026. Relevant public text sections inspected. Research examples, not independently audited outcomes or vetted suppliers. The company locations below are from their own sites/profiles, not incorporation checks.

Blake asked for agencies using an AI-arbitrage model with oil/gas, peptide and similar clients. Separate three claims: a provider markets to the sector; it names an actual client/build; it demonstrably uses external delivery. These do not automatically establish each other. No seller/developer contract, deal margin or commission split was obtained.

## 1. AAI Agency  -  Dallas, Texas  -  oil and gas

- **What it sells:** industry-specific AI training, advice and implementation. Its project catalogue includes an estimating assistant, data-connected agents and planning automation.
- **Plain example:** let an estimator find relevant historical bids and proposals instead of searching manually.
- **External delivery evidence:** the agency's company profile republishes a founder post hiring a Dallas contract engineer to advise and build on-site with a client. This supports use of contract technical delivery; it does not prove a nontechnical owner outsources every project.
- **Limits:** client names and project prices are not disclosed in the inspected catalogue. The founder's industry experience is part of the positioning; not a template proving that a beginner can sell the same technical work immediately.
- **Sources:** [Agency/project catalogue](https://aai.agency/), [US location and contract-engineer hiring post](https://www.linkedin.com/company/aai-agency). Inspected homepage implementation/case sections and profile headquarters/hiring sections. No independent savings audit.

## 2. Ingenia  -  Houston-area office and Mexico City team  -  industrial/offshore services

- **Named buyer:** Aqueos, an underwater-services business working in the oil industry.
- **Problem/build:** replace an old safety-management application with a platform covering hazards, risks, activities and documentation. Agency case explicitly describes strategy, design and build.
- **Model evidence:** public US/Mexico delivery footprint. This establishes a cross-border agency, not subcontracting or an arbitrage margin.
- **AI boundary:** the service page describes AI-assisted safety; the detailed case mainly establishes custom software. Do not invent an autonomous AI safety decision-maker. Reported time savings are projected, not measured realised savings.
- **Sources:** [Aqueos case](https://www.ingenia.com/case-studies/aqueos), [AI services and locations](https://www.ingenia.com/services/ai-solutions). Inspected challenge, solution, impact and office information.

## 3. Cloudtech  -  US headquarters, globally distributed team  -  pharmaceutical enterprise

- **Named buyer:** Eli Lilly, as reported by Cloudtech.
- **Problem/build:** domain specialists manually assemble information from multiple sources for innovation-readiness assessments. Cloudtech describes an eight-agent research/scoring workflow with source evidence and human review.
- **Partner-delivery evidence:** the case explicitly says Cloudtech was brought into an AWS ProServe engagement. This is a concrete example of specialist implementation through a larger delivery relationship. It does not establish which organisation sold the contract, their commercial split, or a simple resale arrangement.
- **Buyer distinction:** a major pharmaceutical company, not a research-peptide ecommerce brand.
- **Source quality:** provider-hosted statement, not independently confirmed by Lilly in this run. The page contains unrelated case blocks below the Lilly section; only the Lilly section is used for these claims. About page calls the HQ New York City and lists a Jersey City hub address; describe it as US-headquartered without inventing a precise office location.
- **Sources:** [Lilly case section](https://cloudtech.com/resources/agentic-ai-powered-innovation-readiness-assessment), [company and partner description](https://cloudtech.com/about-us).

## 4. Toptal  -  specialist talent and consulting model  -  US oil/gas technology buyer

- **Buyer:** unnamed US oil-and-gas technology firm.
- **Problem/build:** make AI analysis of mineral-title records more reliable, using attorney-validated reference answers and regression evaluation.
- **Model relevance:** a talent-network/consulting provider supplying specialist capability. Useful for explaining that the client-facing operator need not personally perform every technical discipline; not a small founder agency or a disclosed wholesale-to-retail resale deal.
- **Limits:** anonymous client, undisclosed price and personnel arrangement. The provider reports error improvements; they are not independent benchmarks or agency financial results.
- **Source:** [Case, service links and freelance-talent offering](https://www.toptal.com/case-study/oil-gas-tech-firm-improves-ai-mineral-title-accuracy). Relevant case text inspected. Included for its US client, not as a newly verified US headquarters claim.

## 5. Convexa  -  San Diego, California  -  peptide/DTC brands

- **What it sells:** managed advertising, creative production/testing, funnels, lifecycle communication and reporting supported by its own software.
- **Peptide evidence:** explicitly lists an unnamed peptide-brand engagement for Meta account management and creative testing.
- **Partner evidence:** says work is delivered directly and through partners, including an Amazon agency and retail-rebate platform. It does not identify the delivery route for that particular peptide client or prove outsourced AI engineering.
- **What students can learn:** a content/marketing agency can specialise in a buyer category and use AI in production. This is different from selling an internal custom AI platform to a peptide manufacturer.
- **Source:** [Agency site](https://convexa.agency/), inspected client examples, partner-delivery sentence, services and location. Client unnamed; illustrative dashboard figures are not financial proof.

## 6. IntuitionLabs  -  San Jose, California  -  pharma specialisation

- **Named buyer:** Scilex Holding Company.
- **Build:** connect claims, prescription and CRM information in embedded Veeva dashboards with automated updates.
- **Useful lesson:** a specialist agency can serve a sophisticated company through a concrete operational implementation, rather than promising scientific drug discovery.
- **Limits:** its own evidence page explicitly says this named case is commercial analytics, not proof of its new AI Acceleration programme. External fulfilment was not established. Keep it as a specialist-implementation example, not confirmed AI arbitrage.
- **Sources:** [Scilex case](https://intuitionlabs.ai/case-studies/veeva-myinsights-pharma-analytics), [evidence boundary and location](https://intuitionlabs.ai/resources/case-studies).

## Teaching use

Best niche-agency example: AAI. Strongest named major-company partner-delivery example: Cloudtech/Lilly. Clearest peptide-marketing/partner example: Convexa, with an anonymous buyer. Ingenia supplies a named industrial software build. None establishes a beginner's exact deal size, profit margin or sales cycle.

Use these to illustrate the business model: choose a buyer category, understand its workflows, sell a defined improvement and organise competent delivery. Carry industry knowledge into diagnosis and use technical specialists for implementation. The free audit is one funnel. Keep the internal fulfilment revenue split out of research and marketing skills.
