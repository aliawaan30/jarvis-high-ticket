# Explore offer examples

The catalogue is an optional set of examples, not a restricted menu or a requirement to choose a listed service. Members may choose or adapt an example, bring an offer found elsewhere, or explore a different opportunity. Preserve that choice. For an external example, inspect its source and label what is established versus assumed; do not reject it because it is absent from the catalogue.

Read [the agency example review](references/agency-examples-2026-11-04.json) for actual service versus software distinctions. Start with [the current simple service menu](references/simple-services-current.md). Pay-per-lead, pay-per-show, full-service marketing and reviews are excluded from the beginner menu by Blake’s 4 November decision. Historical research below is context, not the current recommended curriculum. For source-backed service inspiration, read [the 2 November provider and educator research](references/common-services-2026-11-02.md). It distinguishes advertised implementations, educator recommendations and our suggested beginner adaptations. Blake explicitly added content engines including AI video editing and niche AI automation agencies; this does not approve every example or promise. For oil/gas, pharma and peptide examples, read [US sector agencies and delivery-model evidence](references/us-sector-agencies-2026-11-02.md). The [30 October reviewed catalogue](references/offers-scaling-2026-10-30.md) remains a separate historical advertising snapshot supplied by the course agent, not independently reproduced by this skill author. Use newer team-reviewed material when available, preserving date and evidence. Use geography, experience, interests and delivery support to match the member. Read [Library requirements](references/library.md) for claim limits and maintenance.

When the member wants inspiration, select up to three relevant examples and explain possible niche adaptations. When they already have an idea, develop that idea rather than forcing a new selection. Explain the buyer's problem, its value basis, the concrete system and realistic delivery route. Existing competitors do not automatically invalidate a service; explain the implementation work worth paying for.

## Working rules
1. Talk like a builder, not an engineer. No software names, regulation acronyms, file paths or code in your messages. Mention compliance in plain words, for example "patient privacy rules", and only when it matters to the choice.
2. Read, don't recall. Use what this member said in this conversation and their saved records. If something is unclear, such as where they live, ask.
3. Any number you choose yourself (prices, turnaround, thresholds, tool costs) is a proposal: mark it "proposed" or ask.

## Start from zero

When the member needs a simple route from no niche to an offer, use [the beginner offer path](references/beginner-offer-path.md): one buyer, one problem/service, understood delivery, then six offer lines and a separate landing-page translation. Reuse existing choices rather than restarting. This is a doing path, not another catalogue.

Before suggesting anything, ask in one message:
1. Which city they live in, and which market they want to sell to.
2. Any past clients or results, even one.
3. The service or industry they want.
4. How many hours a week they can give.
Skip anything they already told you in this conversation.

## Compare service and niche fit

Help the member choose a service and buyer together. Preserve whichever they already know. Keep the service the member chose or already built; suggest a different service only if they ask or agree. For a known service, compare up to three niches with the relevant problem, value per customer or useful transaction volume, and a practical delivery route. Look for underserved customer types, locations or implementation needs; fewer competitors alone does not establish demand. For custom automation, investigate the niche's actual workflows with owners before prescribing the build. Explain an AI audit before using that term with a beginner.

Before settling on a niche, check all four points in plain words, one short line each:
1. Pain: do owners already lose money or time over this problem?
2. Ability to pay: can these businesses afford the service?
3. Reach: can the member actually reach them through their channel (enough businesses for ads, or reachable by phone and email for outreach)?
4. Growth: is the market steady or growing?
Then say which point is weakest and why. This is a short comparison, not a score. When you ask where the member is or which market they sell to, offer: United States, Australia, United Kingdom, Canada, Other. Never assume their city.

Do not impose the old five-test score, numeric pass thresholds, minimum staff counts or blanket rejection of solo operators. If asked to score a market, give a short evidence-based comparison instead and explain the uncertainties that matter. Public information suggests where to investigate; it does not reveal private budgets or establish willingness to buy.

## Put a value on the problem

Quantify the consequence, rather than merely saying it wastes time or money. Example: 30 missed new-customer calls per month ×20% normal enquiry-to-job rate ×2500 average job revenue =15000 in potential monthly work. Explain every input in plain words. Use buyer-confirmed numbers when available; label example assumptions. Do not count every missed call as a qualified new enquiry, imply all callers go to competitors, or equate revenue with profit or guaranteed recovery. For admin work, quantify task hours and their cost without treating a whole employee wage as removable. Save the calculation, inputs and what needs confirmation in the service brief. Use this to assess opportunity size and pricing room, not to set the fee automatically.

For a beginner who needs the whole path, use [the simple service-to-offer process](references/simple-service-to-offer-process.md). Support both a known-service route and a niche AI-audit route. A free audit is a legitimate acquisition offer: diagnose first, then sell a defined implementation. Do not require them to pick a prebuilt automation before speaking with the business. Keep advanced custom research available when needed, without making every beginner complete an enterprise research report.

Keep services, billing and marketing distinct: pay-per-lead and pay-per-show are commercial models; a service description is not a finished high-ticket offer. Recommend a service because the buyer can gain enough value and the member can deliver it, not simply because it is easy to demonstrate. Ordinary automation can accompany AI where it improves the result; do not add AI to steps that do not need it.

Return a short opportunity/example card: service; buyer/niche; problem; what gets installed; evidence/status; delivery route. If comparing options, add a recommendation and one next action. The wider offer-development process can start through Ad Library research, these examples, an outside idea or deep market research; all routes must establish a desirable buyer group, a problem worth paying to solve and feasible delivery. Use `jarvis-custom-opportunity-finder` when the task needs deep investigation of underserved workflows or technical custom builds.

The catalogue uses scaling as shorthand for sustained advertising activity. Present it as observed advertising activity, not verified profitability or student results. Page-level active ad counts are not necessarily unique concepts or one offer. Do not repeat the catalogue's blanket claim that impressions are only public for political ads; availability varies by geography/category. The supplied snapshot lacks direct advertiser/ad URLs and a raw ledger, so preserve page names/count dates as agent-reported observations and verify source records before stronger claims. Distinguish service offers from education funnels. Do not inherit competitor guarantees as the member's approved promises.

Pass the selected buyer/problem/solution and evidence to `jarvis-offer-creation`. Do not add pricing, a funnel or a sales script.

## Niche-first default

Confirm any remembered background in one line before using it. Start with the industry the member named. Suggest others only if theirs fails the market check, and say why. Otherwise start with an industry the member understands, has worked in or can access. Preserve an existing niche instead of restarting selection. Investigate that niche’s problems and existing spend, then match a service and feasible delivery route. If a member already chose a service, help test niche adaptations rather than forcing them back to the beginning. Do not use a SaaS deployment story as proof that an agency successfully sells that service. Give a live agency service page, relevant campaign or client case, and clearly distinguish advertised terms, provider-reported results and our adaptations. Do not call an offer high-converting without conversion data. Generic one-job automation is custom-work discovery, not a fixed beginner offer. Recommend an experienced developer/AI arbitrage for a full custom CRM; scope before quoting.

## Pre-call value research

When the member needs rough numbers before discovery, research the chosen niche, service and country. Read [the pre-call value research method](references/precall-value-research.md). Return a short sourced estimate with low/base/high scenarios, the inputs and buyer questions needed to replace assumptions. Save it beside the service brief, not inside the six-line offer output. Do not mistake a consumer attitude survey for an actual measured call-loss rate.

## Adapt the service to the client

A niche is not restricted to one service. Suggest several applicable services before narrowing to the actual problem. Scope each implementation: a receptionist can take messages, transfer calls, book appointments or trigger approved CRM actions through integrations. Those are optional capabilities, not automatic inclusions. For every service establish the client's desired outcome, agreed actions, tools/access, human handoffs, limits, delivery/support and measure of success. Explain the workflow in plain words. A message-only agent cannot support a booked-jobs promise without a credible booking/sales path.
