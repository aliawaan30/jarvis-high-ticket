# Separate AI implementation demonstrations

Deliver these in their own file or link, outside the short proposal. A proposal may include a simple optional link; it does not embed this walkthrough.

Choose the pattern matching the actual service. These are storytelling structures, not claims that the integrations exist. Use the buyer's tools only when their capabilities are verified; otherwise label the connection proposed.

## Request → draft → approval
Suitable for order intake, quote preparation and document processing.

Show an example inbound email/document alongside the structured draft. Highlight the extracted details, then one missing or uncertain field. A staff member resolves it and approves the next action. Finish on the useful output and its record in the existing system. Never equate plausible extracted text with verified source accuracy.

## Message → response → handoff
Suitable for lead response, reactivation and follow-up.

Show an eligible example conversation, relevant reply and owner handoff. Show an opt-out or ambiguous answer stopping or escalating the sequence. Finish with the assigned action and history in the CRM. A booked call is not a sale; demonstrate downstream outcome tracking separately if included.

## Disconnected work → one project record
Suitable for operational CRMs and bounded custom apps.

Show a specific job moving from request to assigned work and completion, with the key information in one record. Demonstrate who can change its status and where an exception is recorded. Avoid a dashboard full of fictional revenue and performance metrics. Do not confuse a clickable front end with working persistence and permissions.

## Information → suggested answer → source
Suitable for internal knowledge assistants and support triage.

Ask one practical question. Show the suggested answer, supporting source and a path to a human when evidence is missing. Show the access boundary relevant to that buyer. Do not sell arbitrary accuracy percentages or imply the model is the source of truth.

## Demo design contract

Keep the main sequence to 3–5 visible steps and roughly 30–60 seconds of explanation. Let the presenter step manually or replay. Add one exception rather than multiple impressive happy paths. Provide a static final state or screenshots for sending. Put the demonstration status beside its title. If connected to real tools, use a sandbox or authorized test records; presentation controls must not accidentally message clients or change production data.
