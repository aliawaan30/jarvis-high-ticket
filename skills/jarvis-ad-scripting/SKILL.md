# Jarvis Ad Scripting

Draft, 29 October 2026. Built for Module 4 of the high ticket course. The member records these on a phone, talking to camera. Write for a voice, not a page.

## Purpose

Turn one offer into a set of ads that each test a different way of talking about it. The market picks the winner. The member then records versions of the winner. Every script must be sayable by a real person on a real call, aimed at one named kind of business owner, and end in the same place: the booking page.

## Inputs, read before writing

1. The one page offer from the offer module: who it is for, what gets installed, what it fixes, what it is worth to them.
2. The market research brief: where this market loses money, the words buyers use for the problem, what they have already tried.
3. The buyer's awareness stage, as the member states it. Problem aware buyers get problem led hooks. Solution aware buyers get mechanism and objection led hooks. If the member does not know, ask once, then default to problem aware.
4. The booking page link, or a placeholder if it is not built yet.
5. Any real proof the member has: a named client result with permission, a screenshot they own, a number they can stand behind. If there is none, say so and do not write a proof script.

If an input is missing, ask for it in one line. Do not invent it.

## Output

Between five and ten scripts. Default eight. Each script is labelled with the angle it tests, in this format:

```
SCRIPT 3 of 8 · ANGLE: the objection ("we already have a CRM")
Length: about 45 seconds
Who it is aimed at: [the exact business owner, in one line]

[the words, spoken, in short lines]

CTA: [one sentence, same for every script]
```

Spread the scripts across these angles. Use each at least once if the inputs support it.

- **Costly problem.** Lead with the thing that is costing them money right now, in their words, with the consequence attached. "Every quote that sits in your inbox for two days is a job going to the bloke who answered first."
- **Desired result.** Lead with what their week looks like after. Concrete and small: a phone that gets answered, a Friday without chasing invoices. Never "transform your business".
- **The objection.** Open with the exact thing they are already thinking, agree with it, then turn it. "You have tried software before and it made more work. Fair. Here is why this is different."
- **Mechanism.** Show how it works in three plain steps, so a sceptic can picture it. No AI jargon. Say what happens to a job from the moment it comes in.
- **Proof.** Only with real, permitted proof. One result, with the context, said plainly. If there is none, replace with a second costly problem script on a different pain.

## Script rules

- **Hook in the first three seconds.** The first line names the person or the pain. "If you run a plumbing business with more than five vans" or "Your office is drowning in quotes". Never start with hello, a name, or "AI is changing everything".
- **30 to 60 seconds spoken.** Around 75 to 150 words. Short lines, one thought per line, written the way people talk.
- **Plain language.** No "leverage", "streamline", "cutting edge", "solution". If the member would not say it on the phone to a client, cut it.
- **One idea per script.** One angle, one pain, one picture of the fix.
- **Same CTA every time.** One sentence that sends them to the booking page and says what happens there: a short call to see whether this fits their business. Never "buy now", never urgency, never a discount.
- **No price.** Not the build price, not the monthly, not "from $". Pricing lives on the call, against the cost of their problem. This is a programme rule from the member's marketing preferences.
- **No invented results.** No "clients save 20 hours a week" unless the member supplied that result with permission. Illustrations are fine when marked as such in the spoken line: "imagine a Monday where".
- **No fake testimonials, no fake screenshots, no implied client lists.**
- **No em dashes anywhere in the output.**
- **Write for recording.** Add a one line note on delivery where it helps: "slow down on this line", "smile here", "look at the lens for the CTA".

## Variation mode

When the member pastes a winning script and its numbers and asks for versions:

1. Keep the angle, the core message and the CTA exactly the same.
2. Produce five versions, changing one thing each, and label the change:
   - a new opening line
   - a different story or example in the middle
   - a different objection handled inside the same angle
   - a shorter cut, 20 to 30 seconds
   - a version written to be filmed somewhere else (in the ute, on site, walking)
3. Do not drift into a new angle. If the member wants new angles, that is a fresh batch, not a variation.

## Review before handing over

Read every script aloud in your head as the business owner. Reject and rewrite any script that:

- takes more than three seconds to say who it is for,
- could be about any business rather than this one,
- contains a number the member did not supply,
- contains a price, a discount or urgency,
- sounds like an ad rather than a person.

Finish with a one line note on which two scripts to record first and why, based on the awareness stage.
