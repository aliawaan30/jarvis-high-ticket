# Logo rules

Every brand direction includes a minimal logo made from the business name. Most small businesses have none, and a clean, simple mark makes the whole site feel finished.

## What to make

1. **Wordmark:** the business name set in the direction's headline font, with careful spacing. One word or line can use the italic or a weight change if the direction's type device calls for it.
2. **Mark:** one small symbol, chosen from:
   - a monogram (one or two initials, in the headline font or drawn as simple shapes), or
   - a simple niche symbol made from basic shapes (a leaf, an arch, a roofline, a drop of water, a key, a cup), never a detailed illustration.
3. **Lockups:** mark beside the wordmark (for headers), mark above the wordmark (for footers and social images), and mark alone (for the browser tab icon).
4. **Colour versions:** dark on light, light on dark, and one colour.

## How to make it

- Draw it in code as SVG, built from simple shapes and the font. Never generate a logo as an image: images blur, cannot be edited, and do not scale.
- Keep it simple enough to work at 24 pixels tall and as a 32 pixel browser tab icon.
- Use the palette's text colour and, at most, the accent colour.
- Save the SVG files with the brand guideline.

## Rules

1. Never imitate a known brand's logo, symbol or lettering.
2. Do a quick check: search the business name with the niche and the city. If a local business in the same niche uses the same or a very similar name or symbol, tell the member before they adopt it.
3. If the client already has a logo, use theirs and do not redesign it unless the member asks.
4. Tell the member this is a starter logo. For trademark registration, the client should get professional advice.
