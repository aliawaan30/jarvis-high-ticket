# Rule packs

Starting points for the Rules layer. Rules change: every time, confirm with a fresh search, record the date, and say clearly when something could not be verified. You are not a lawyer; when a rule is unclear, recommend the client checks with their own advisor and list the question.

## How to research rules for any niche

1. Search "[niche] advertising rules [state] [year]" and "[regulator] advertising rules".

2. Find the regulator (state board, bar, licensing body) and read its advertising rule, not only summaries.

3. Check the FTC's rules on endorsements, testimonials and reviews (US): no fake or incentivised reviews presented as genuine, typical-results claims need support, material connections must be disclosed.

4. Check privacy: if a form collects health, financial or legal details, keep the form to contact details and the reason for enquiry, and say so in the privacy notice.

5. Record each rule with its source and date in the brief.

## Medical aesthetics (med spa), Texas. Checked 18 November 2026.

1. **No guarantees or risk-minimising claims.** Prohibited: guarantees of results, "risk-free", statements that minimise complications, "permanent" when maintenance is needed, digitally altered before and after images. Source: Phorest, Texas med spa compliance checklist 2026, https://www.phorest.com/us/blog/compliance-checklist-for-med-spas-in-texas-2026-edition/

2. **Before and after photos need separate written consent** for advertising use; general treatment consent is not enough. Source: Med Spa Standards, Texas advertising compliance 2026, https://medspastandards.com/blog/texas/texas-med-spa-advertising-compliance-fda-ftc

3. **The physician owns the advertising.** Every physician is responsible for the form and content of advertising for their practice and is deemed to have reviewed and approved it; this cannot be delegated to an agency (22 TAC Chapter 164). So: the medical director signs off the site content before launch. Source: Med Spa Standards, Texas compliance checklist, https://medspastandards.com/blog/texas/texas-med-spa-compliance-checklist

4. **Titles are claims.** "Medical director" and "physician-supervised" have legal content; describe credentials exactly. Only physicians may present themselves as medical doctors. Sources: Med Spa Standards policy manual guide, https://medspastandards.com/blog/med-spa-policy-procedure-manual ; Phorest checklist above.

5. **IV therapy (HB 3749, Jenifer's Law, effective 1 September 2025):** elective IV therapy may only be administered by physicians, PAs, APRNs and RNs. If the client offers IV therapy, the site must not imply otherwise. Source: Decoda Health, https://decodahealth.com/blog/new-med-spa-laws-2026

6. **TMB Rule 169.28 (effective 9 January 2025):** non-surgical cosmetic procedures are the practice of medicine; delegation must be in writing, with physician disclosure at the facility. Source: Med Spa Standards, Texas regulatory changes 2026, https://medspastandards.com/blog/texas/texas-med-spa-regulatory-changes-2026

7. **What patients look for (customer layer, for reference):** provider credentials and staff bios, consistent recent reviews, before and after photos, clear pricing with no hidden fees, follow-up care. Source: American Society of Plastic Surgeons, https://www.plasticsurgery.org/patient-safety/how-to-choose-the-right-medical-spa-for-you

**Site consequences:** a providers section with real licences; a consented, unaltered gallery only; "from" prices where the client agrees; real reviews only; no banned words anywhere, including image text and alt text; medical director sign-off as a launch gate.

## Other regulated niches: always research before writing

- **Dental:** the state dental board's advertising rules, especially who may call themselves a "specialist", plus before and after consent and testimonials.

- **Legal:** the state bar's lawyer advertising rules (disclaimers, "specialist" claims, past results).

- **Financial and insurance:** the regulator's advertising rules and required disclosures.

- **Contractors and roofing:** state and city licensing (some states license roofers, some do not), insurance claims advice limits, warranty wording.

- **Real estate:** brokerage disclosure and fair housing rules.
