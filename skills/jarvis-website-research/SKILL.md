# Website research

A premium site is decided by research, not decoration. Every section on the final site must trace back to a finding in this brief. If a section has no reason, it does not go on the site.

Module 7. Before this skill, the member should have a client (signed or a serious prospect) in their Agency HQ. If there is no client yet, research still works for a sample site, but say so on the brief.

## Inside the website builder

When jarvis-premium-website runs this skill as part of its one-prompt flow, skip the intake (it has already asked), run the six layers, save the brief, return the short summary, and let the website flow continue. Do not show the hand-over line.

## 1. Intake: one message, then stop

Read what you already know first: the client record in Agency HQ (business name, niche, city, website, deal notes), the scoping notes, and memory. Ask only what is missing, in one message, numbered, with a blank line between questions. Use [references/intake.md](references/intake.md) for the full question bank and how to shorten it.

The core questions, when unknown:

1. The business name, what it does, and its city and neighbourhood.

2. The one action the site must drive (calls, bookings, quote requests, purchases).

3. Who the best customers are, in the client's words.

4. Existing brand: logo, colours, fonts, photos, or "nothing yet".

5. What the client already has: real reviews, credentials, prices, before and after photos with consent, team photos.

6. Two or three websites the client likes, and what they like about them.

7. Anything the client must have, or must never have.

Accept "not sure" and move on. Never block on a question the research can answer.

## 2. Research: six layers

Search the web for each layer. Prefer primary and authoritative sources (regulators, professional bodies, the competitors' own sites) over listicles. Note the source of every finding. Spend about 10 to 15 searches for a normal brief; more only for a regulated niche.

1. **Customer.** How buyers in this niche choose: what they check, fear and compare. Look for professional bodies, consumer guides and surveys.

2. **Rules.** What this business is legally allowed to say and show, in its state and country. Check [references/rule-packs.md](references/rule-packs.md) first. A pack is a starting point, never final: rules change, so confirm the current rules with a fresh search every time, and record the date. Regulated niches (health, dental, medical aesthetics, legal, financial, real estate, contractors) always get this layer in full.

3. **Market.** Three to five local competitors: what they offer, charge and claim, how their sites are structured, what they do well, what they miss. Note local positioning (neighbourhoods, customer types). Never copy their words, photos or claims.

4. **Search.** What people actually type: service plus city phrases, questions, and comparisons. Use search suggestions, "people also ask" style questions and competitor page titles as evidence. Do not invent search volumes.

5. **Client facts.** What is true about this business, from the intake and the client's own materials. Anything not confirmed by the client goes in [brackets] and is never written as fact.

6. **Design.** How the best websites in this niche look and work. Start from the niche's profile in [references/niche-design-profiles.md](references/niche-design-profiles.md), then study 5 to 8 strong sites: the local leaders from the market layer and well-known national or award-winning ones in the same niche. Record what buyers expect to see, the photo style, the layouts and image patterns that fit, the conversion features, and what would look wrong for this niche. Learn from structure and feel only; never copy words, photos, logos or identity. Write it as the niche design profile in the brief; the brand guide and the website skills follow it.

## Reference sites

If the member or client names a website they love, study it as part of the Design layer: its structure, section order, type devices, image and motion techniques, and what makes it feel premium. Write what to borrow (structure and techniques) and what never to copy (its words, photos, logo, colours and identity). Without a screen recording you cannot see its motion; ask the member for a 30 second scroll recording if motion matters, or describe motion as unknown.

## 3. The research brief

Write it in the shape of [references/brief-template.md](references/brief-template.md). It always contains:

1. **The business and the one action.**

2. **Findings per layer,** each with its source and date, including the niche design profile (what fits and what to avoid).

3. **The site plan:** every section, with the finding that justifies it.

4. **Must never appear:** banned claims and content for this niche and state.

5. **Content needed from the client:** a checklist (credentials, prices, photos with consent, reviews, licence numbers), each marked who provides it.

6. **Sign-offs required before launch:** for example, a Texas med spa's medical director must approve the content.

7. **Open questions.**

Save it in Jarvis's Data area as the client's website research, with the date. Then show the member a short summary in chat: the top five findings, the site plan as a numbered list, and the content checklist. Offer the full brief.

## 4. Hand over

End with exactly one next step: "Next, I'll turn this into three brand directions. Send: Use the brand guide skill to create three brand directions from my research."

## Rules

1. Never invent facts about the client: credentials, prices, years, reviews, awards, team members, licences or results. Use [brackets] until the client confirms.

2. Never copy a competitor's copy, photos, logos or claims. Learn from structure and positioning only.

3. Every finding has a source. If you could not verify something, say so.

4. Rule packs are starting points. Always confirm the current rules with a fresh search, and date them.

5. Plain words, short lines, a blank line between separate lines. No log lines.

6. Research the client's niche and city, not a generic version of it.
