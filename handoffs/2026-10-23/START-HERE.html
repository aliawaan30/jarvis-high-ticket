The student journey

  1. Find an opportunity: research niche, recurring problem, service and offer together. Simple means understandable and demonstrable, not low-value or restricted to websites.
  2. Prepare and decide: student completes prework; coach strategy call refines the offer; technical CSM checks feasibility, costs and delivery ownership.
  3. Prepare acquisition: approved offer feeds buyer research, scripts, VSL, landing page, statics, editing, qualification, booking and pre-call messaging.
  4. Win clients: choose ads and/or targeted calls/outreach appropriate to reachable buyers. Review actual calls and objections.
  5. Deliver: sold deal becomes a project with scoped milestones, access, acceptance tests and support ownership.
  6. Learn: capture objections, campaign data, delivery issues and human feedback; update the relevant skill or offer with versioned decisions.

Delivery can use existing software, templates, scoped custom builds or a developer. The optional internal technical-team fulfilment arrangement is a separate commercial decision, not part of the research/marketing skills.

Course content is the teaching layer: explain the problem, demonstrate the workflow, have students practise and review their work. Skills help execute that knowledge; a collection of prompts does not replace the teaching or technical support.

How the system should flow

Offer to acquisition

Student profile → research options → offer draft → coach and technical review → approved offer version → buyer angles → scripts, VSL and pages → human review → published test → results and objections → revision.

Footage to ads

Jarvis receives uploaded footage → Creative opens an editing job using the approved script and style → editor runtime transcribes and renders → automatic checks plus visual/audio review → student receives draft in chat or Telegram → revision or approval of that exact version → Meta Ads receives the approved asset and campaign plan → publication within separately agreed permissions → campaign ID and results saved.

Approving a script is not approving the rendered asset or ad spend. A revised asset invalidates its earlier publication approval. A failure returns to the same job, not a fresh unrelated conversation.

Targeted outbound

Offer-fit rules → business accounts → buyer/decision-maker → sourced contact routes → account research → email/call drafts → review → authorised sender → replies and suppression → booked call brief → call review → won/lost.

Sale to delivery

Won deal and agreed scope → project → access and sample data → build milestones → acceptance checks → client handover → monitoring/support. Context and files stay attached to the project. Technical CSM resolves uncertain scope before commitments.

Voice and memory

A phone request should become the same durable task record as a typed request. Voice acknowledges intake; completion requires the worker’s output and checks. Cross-session memory comes from maintained records and retrieved context, not an assumption that every model remembers every chat.

Shared records and the CRM

Jarvis needs one business record, not disconnected chats. The custom CRM is the student’s operating interface; its build is not established by the skills in this package.

  • Today: next actions, meetings, overdue follow-ups and blockers.
  • Pipeline: leads, contacted, booked, attended, proposal, won/lost; each stage has a real event.
  • Contacts and accounts: company, buyer, source, problem, qualification, conversation history, contact provenance and suppression.
  • Calls: recording/transcript references, notes, objections, reviews and next steps.
  • Delivery projects: agreed scope, access status, milestones, ownership, questions, acceptance checks and updates.
  • Performance: spend, qualified enquiries, bookings, attendance, closes and collected cash. Keep setup revenue and recurring revenue distinct.
  • Agent activity: active task, owner, status, blocked reason, source inputs, output versions and approvals.

Suggested records: StudentProfile, Business, OfferVersion, Campaign, Account, Contact, Conversation, Appointment, Deal, Project, Task, AgentRun, AssetVersion, Approval, Event and Suppression. These are a proposed contract, not an already deployed schema.

Task lifecycle: queued → running → needs review / blocked / failed → approved or complete. Persist dependencies, attempt count, event IDs and output references. Retrying must not duplicate sends, bookings or campaigns. Only report success when the action has a verifiable receipt. Preserve tenant separation and keep each student’s integrations and credentials isolated.

Product and quality decisions

  • Plain-English buyer, pain, solution, outcome and demonstration. Students must understand the system deeply enough to sell it.
  • Keep simple opportunity finding separate from advanced implementation discovery.
  • Source claims, show numerical assumptions and compare native software. A business’s total problem cost is not automatically recoverable value.
  • Lead public copy with the desired result, then the mechanism. No public offer pricing, deposits or dollar fee waivers.
  • Work-free terms need a defined outcome, timeframe, scope, responsibilities and costs. Do not invent a revenue promise.
  • Use inspected reference hierarchy; do not substitute generic SaaS pages. No borrowed testimonials or artificial scarcity.
  • Qualification questions one at a time; full name, email and phone together at the end; no time estimate. Qualified-for-call page leads to calendar, then actual booked confirmation.
  • Four static concepts must test distinct creative angles, not recolours. Direct callout/story-style is an important format.
  • Separate research quality, offer quality, visual quality, functional QA and integration QA. A passing browser test is not proof of a winning offer.
  • Capture feedback as scoped decisions, with evidence and version. Test one complete example before multiplying it.

Testing plan for the technical partner

  1. Clean workspace: use a fictional student, separate data and a fresh chat. Load only the selected skill and its actual references. Record model/platform, skill hash, prompt and tool access.
  2. Research: test a beginner, an experienced seller and a technical builder. Score understandable service, real buyer need, alternatives, feasibility, economics, audience and acquisition route. Missing evidence must remain missing.
  3. Offer: choose one viable option with human review. Save one approved version. Test whether later assets use that exact offer without changing terms.
  4. Creative and funnel: produce VSL storyboard, reference-led page, booking/prep flow and four statics. Check actual images and mobile pages, not just generated text or screenshots existing.
  5. Editing: one short synthetic or authorised clip first. Check cuts, captions, rendering, revision loop, download and large-file failure handling.
  6. Orchestration: assign a dependency chain; change the offer; verify stale assets are flagged. Retry a failed task, restart a worker and duplicate an event. Outputs must stay linked and side effects must not repeat.
  7. Connected actions: sandbox leads/calendar/sender/ad account. Test opt-out, reply pause, cancellation, reschedule, duplicate events, permissions and owner handoff before any real campaign.
  8. Student pilot: one student can explain the service, operate the tools and obtain technical help without Blake rewriting every output. Commercial results require real buyer testing, not just internal quality scores.
