## 1. What the member buys

**Proposed offer sentence:** “We help you build and sell AI solutions to businesses, with your own Jarvis AI team, a clear client-acquisition system and hands-on strategy and technical support from choosing the offer through client delivery.”

The desired result is a functioning AI service business: an understandable service, prospective buyers, sales conversations and the ability to deliver what was sold. The mechanisms are Jarvis 2.0, skills, strategy sessions, technical scoping, demos/proposals, call reviews and community. The course organises those mechanisms into a usable sequence.

The commercial difference from ordinary course access is **help making the important decisions and completing real work**. The strategy session settles buyer/problem/offer; technical support turns it into an implementable scope; Jarvis helps produce the research, acquisition assets, demo and proposal; reviews improve actual execution. We should show these handoffs in the programme walkthrough.

## 3. Acquisition: where I agree and disagree

**Module 3: Client acquisition / outreach. Module 4: Paid ads.** Both are core course content. Omnichannel is part of the offer's positioning. I withdraw the obsolete framing that targeted outbound is only an optional niche extra.

I favour targeted outbound plus calls as the default first test for a beginner, because it gives direct buyer feedback with a controllable initial list. I disagree that ads require a closed deal first. A member with an understood problem, credible demo, delivery route and test budget can test ads before a sale. A member who already has clients or an audience may start there. Preserve Blake's deletion of “Do Not Run Ads Yet”; give a practical readiness decision instead of a sale-count gate.

Your account scoring is useful, but the current lesson overstates what it establishes:

- A job ad, unanswered-call review or missing booking form is a **signal to investigate**. It does not prove costly pain or purchase intent. Write “three signals that suggest fit” rather than “prove the pain.”
- “Running ads with no system behind them” normally cannot be known from public pages. Record the visible evidence and the discovery question separately.
- A company register gives an entity or officeholder, not necessarily the operational budget owner. Headcount is a starting hypothesis, not a rule that the owner always decides below ten staff.
- Do not claim “nobody else does this” or that scoring changes a 2% reply rate without a comparative test. We have not established either.
- “Jarvis runs stages 1–5; the member only does stage 6” is ahead of our demonstrated execution. Initially the member reviews account fit, buyer identity and enrichment accuracy as well. Tool access, provider coverage, time and cost need a real run before claiming 500 accounts in an afternoon.
- “One message, five channels” should mean one consistent problem/angle adapted to each channel. An identical pitch everywhere will read mechanically. Replies, bookings and opt-outs stop the sequence across all channels. SMS belongs where contact permission and campaign rules support its use; a scraped mobile alone is not a completed sending decision.

**Recommended week-one exercise:** install Jarvis and CRM; prepare one buyer/problem hypothesis; choose it with the coach; map a demonstrable solution and delivery route; produce 30 candidate accounts; manually review the first ten; confirm likely buyers and available contact methods; approve channel-specific copy; run a small first call/email/LinkedIn batch and log real objections. Thirty is an exercise size, not an evidence-backed conversion threshold. Prepare paid-ad creative and funnel in parallel for members ready to test them.

Track the actual funnel: accounts → contactable buyers → conversations → call ones booked → attended → qualified → call twos → won → cash collected. Your seven-call-ones-to-two-deals example assumes about 29% conversion before accounting for attendance/qualification. Keep it as an adjustable scenario. A simple numerical model should reveal the assumptions, then be replaced with observed rates.

The $22k CRM is the best available starting story; use actual records where available and mark reconstructed stages. Then record the acquisition lab.
