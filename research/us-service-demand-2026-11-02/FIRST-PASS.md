## Assessment of the preceding beginner tests

- Landscape extra-work handoff: understandable entry implementation/process project, not established as a high-ticket AI flagship. Existing Buildxact functionality overlaps; actual omissions and willingness to pay are unknown.
- Distributor order drafting: more substantial operational automation with greater technical demands; stronger discovery hypothesis, better suited initially to focused account outreach than assuming cold Meta acquisition. Target volume and willingness to pay remain unmeasured.
- Supplier-price preparation: specialised operational implementation, rejected as high-ticket recommendation at the tested assumptions because native/product alternatives overlap and low-volume capacity value is insufficient.

Narrowness alone is not the problem. Reachable buyers, value, repeatable delivery, implementation cost and acquisition economics determine leverage. These runs did not establish niche prevalence or a scalable acquisition channel.

## Better next research pass

Study service families, not tiny workflows first: inbound response/booking; old-lead/customer reactivation; maintenance renewals/seasonal booking; ad-to-booked-appointment systems; niche CRM with a specific operational result; deeper document/order workflow implementations.

For each, capture advertiser IDs/dates/current page counts/actual distinct angles and destination; classify service vs SaaS vs education; find named deployments and buyer-origin evidence; compare native software/vendor setup; assess reachable buyer numbers; model both client and student economics; specify primary acquisition channel and a small buyer validation test. Sustained ads and fresh variants justify investigation, not a profitability declaration.

Demand signals to combine: current paid providers and independently identifiable customers; buyer reviews describing unresolved work; recent hiring/RFPs for the workflow; specific purchase-intent searches; repeated competitor advertising and offer evolution; distribution/partner implementations; buyer discovery and paid pilot/deposit. Each measures a different thing. Hiring is spend on a problem, not intent to buy AI. A pilot is stronger than praise, but repeatable acquisition/delivery/retention remains to test.

Course implication: support both a readily understood repeatable outcome offer and an advanced custom implementation path. Do not confuse a technical research niche with the best default offer for a beginner buying clients through Meta. Equally, do not ban native-software implementations where meaningful buyer value and specialist work actually exist.
