5. AI appointment setting and opportunity recovery

What it is. A system that responds to enquiries, asks the right questions and helps interested buyers take the next step. Appointment setting is one part. The business wants more sold work and cash collected, so the system must connect to what happens after the booking too.

ServiceWho it contactsWhat the business wants
New-lead conversionSomeone who just enquiredReach them, qualify the job and book the right appointment
Unsold-opportunity recoverySomeone who enquired or received a quote but never boughtReopen suitable opportunities and turn more into paid work
Past-customer reactivationSomeone who already boughtBring them back for another project or service

An offer you can adapt: recover opportunities they already paid for

The business has already spent money attracting the buyer, answering questions and preparing a quote. Then the buyer goes quiet. Some have bought elsewhere. Others still need help deciding. Your service finds the opportunities still worth pursuing and gives the sales team a useful way back into the conversation.

General offer direction: We help [type of business] turn [eligible unsold enquiries or quotes] into [the paid work they want], using a managed follow-up and recovery system.

Choose the niche by checking whether it has valuable quoted work, enough eligible opportunities, reachable decision-makers and capacity to deliver additional jobs. Roofing is our worked example, not the only market you can choose.

Worked example: unsold roofing estimates

We help residential roofing companies turn unsold roof estimates into sold jobs and cash collected through a managed estimate-recovery system.

The owner's pain. They paid for the enquiry. A rep visited the property and prepared an estimate. Now the quote is sitting open, the rep is chasing newer leads, and nobody knows whether the homeowner is still considering the roof or has a question holding them back.

What you install and manage. Connect the agreed CRM records, select the eligible quotes, prepare approved text/email follow-up, handle routine replies, hand questions to the right rep, arrange the next appointment and track what becomes sold and paid. The roofer approves prices, answers technical questions, closes and delivers the job.

  1. Choose the right quotes. Remove sold jobs, duplicates, firm refusals and people who should not be contacted. Keep active sales conversations with their current owner.
  2. Restart the conversation. Refer to the actual estimate and find out whether the homeowner still needs help.
  3. Handle the next step. Use approved answers for routine questions. Send pricing, scope and technical decisions to the roofer.
  4. Get the rep involved. Book the appropriate review or callback, with the original quote and conversation attached.
  5. Follow the result. Record attended appointments, sold jobs and cash collected. A reply or booking is not a paid roof.

Real example. Hatch's Bone Dry Roofing case reports over $7 million closed from rehash and follow-up in a year, with four people operating the workflow across 17 instances. This is a large company's reported result, not your proof or a result to promise a new client.
