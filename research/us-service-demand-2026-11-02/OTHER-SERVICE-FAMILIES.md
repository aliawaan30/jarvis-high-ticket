# Genuinely different services beyond customer conversations

2 November 2026. Relevant primary case text opened and inspected. These are service directions supported by deployments, not proof of an underserved niche, profitable Meta campaign or a beginner's ability to charge high-ticket fees. All performance claims below are provider-reported. Illustrative student packages are our adaptations, not the exact services purchased in the case.

## 1. Estimating and drawing-change workflow

**Buyer/problem:** a contractor's estimators repeatedly measure plans, reconcile drawing revisions and rebuild quantities before pricing a bid. Slow estimating limits how many suitable jobs they can pursue.

**What the business gets:** AI-assisted takeoff, a reviewed quantity output, change comparison and a handoff into its estimating process. The estimator still checks measurements, specification, rates, exclusions and the price.

**Concrete offer direction:** “Help your estimating team get more accurate bids out without adding another estimator.” A bounded first service covers one trade, drawing type and estimating platform; no blanket accuracy or extra-win guarantee.

**Opened example:** Coastal Construction / Togal.AI reports early pricing for a typical 28–30 storey project fell from 32–40 hours to 13–20 hours. It is a large Florida contractor, not a small-trade benchmark. The vendor explicitly acknowledges other process and business changes contributed to company-level financial improvements. https://www.togal.ai/case-study/coastal-construction-65-percent-faster-with-togal-ai

**Commercial question:** can an experienced estimator plus technical implementer improve this particular buyer's existing workflow beyond ordinary Togal onboarding? The tool exists; subscription resale alone is not a differentiated implementation. Better for students with construction understanding or a domain partner. Direct outreach and trade partnerships are the first channel hypothesis, not a validated Meta acquisition plan.

## 2. Invoice and purchasing reconciliation

**Buyer/problem:** finance/purchasing teams copy bills and compare invoices or supplier confirmations against properties, orders and agreed terms. Staff repeat the work across different files and systems; errors create rework or incorrect records.

**What the business gets:** document intake, extraction, approved vendor/property/order mapping, duplicate and mismatch checks, human exceptions and a checked accounting/ERP handoff. Paying invoices is a separate controlled action, not an automatic result of reading one.

**Concrete offer direction:** “Get the bills into the right records without your team retyping every document.” A purchasing variant is “Flag supplier confirmations that don't match what you ordered.” These are related back-office systems, not two entirely different technologies.

**Opened examples:** Hometown Holdings, Virginia, manages 2,500 units; Nanonets describes email-to-Rent Manager utility bill processing and reports 88% straight-through processing. Augeo, Memphis, uses an invoice pipeline for an unnamed accounting client and reports daily entry fell from four hours to under 30 minutes. UniPro's supplier-confirmation case describes matching against Dynamics 365; the page also contains generic Salesforce/invoice sections unrelated to its core story, so do not use those sections as confirmation of UniPro's exact architecture.

Sources: https://nanonets.com/customers/hometown-holdings ; https://nanonets.com/customers/augeo ; https://nanonets.com/customers/unipro

**Commercial question:** enough volume, available permissions, exact accounting rules and a meaningful native gap. Bank-detail changes, payment release and exception decisions require the buyer's controls. A supported implementation for a property portfolio or accounting provider is more credible than promising “all finance automated.” Likely targeted outbound/partner-led sale; channel untested.

## 3. Internal company knowledge assistant

**Buyer/problem:** employees repeatedly search manuals, policy folders, product spreadsheets and old messages or interrupt a few knowledgeable people for answers. This is internal work, distinct from an ecommerce sales chatbot.

**What the business gets:** permission-aware access to approved knowledge, answers tied to source material, a clear route for missing/conflicting information, and integration with a tool the team already uses. Documents need owners and updating; uploading a folder is not a complete service.

**Concrete offer direction:** “Give your team a reliable way to find the right answer from your own company information.” Start with one department and question set; judge answer quality and successful task completion, not chatbot message count.

**Opened example:** Microsoft describes Regal Rexnord's internal Teams/M365 agent answering over 2,000 questions monthly, with an estimated 2,400 staff hours released annually. A separate assistant helps product-service representatives retrieve information. This is an enterprise of over 30,000 employees, not proof of small-business budgets or a beginner result. https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/26197-regal-rexnord-microsoft-copilot-studio/

**Commercial question:** existing Copilot/search may suffice. The service is useful where knowledge preparation, permissions, system connections and ongoing answer quality require specialist work. A generic chat wrapper faces direct SaaS competition. Advanced implementation path; start with a specific team rather than “Jarvis for everything.”

## 4. Brief-to-proposal production

**Buyer/problem:** a sales team repeatedly searches a product catalogue and assembles custom presentations with product details, visuals, prices and client context. Opportunities wait while staff build documents by hand.

**What the business gets:** structured brief intake, catalogue/availability lookup, product selection, a branded draft presentation and human approval of suitability, pricing and claims before sending. Different from an AI setter: the output is the actual sales document.

**Concrete offer direction:** “Turn a customer brief into a proposal your salesperson can review and send in minutes.” Pick one repeatable proposal type and an approved catalogue, not arbitrary bids or contracts.

**Opened example:** n8n describes PromptGorillas implementing BeGlobal's corporate-gifting proposal workflow with custom frontend, product data and Google Slides. It reports an under-one-minute machine workflow and an estimated near-tenfold increase in proposal capacity. BeGlobal is Dutch, so this is evidence of an implemented service, not US-specific demand. Human review time and machine runtime are distinct. https://n8n.io/case-studies/beglobal/

**Commercial question:** enough bespoke proposal volume and a catalogue that can be kept accurate. Existing quoting/CRM proposal tools are the alternatives. AI-generated imagery must not misrepresent the product being sold. Technical/creative support likely needed; promising faster preparation is more supportable than promising extra closed sales.

## 5. New-client onboarding and project activation

**Buyer/problem:** after a sale, someone repeatedly collects information, checks missing items, creates records, sets up the project and coordinates handoffs. The client pays but waits for useful work to start.

**What the business gets:** approved intake, completeness checking, structured records/project creation, assigned tasks and an exception queue. Sensitive access and permission changes follow authorised procedures; credentials should not be pasted into an AI chat.

**Concrete offer direction:** “Get new clients from signed agreement to ready-to-start without the repeated admin chase.” First scope is one product/customer type and known setup steps.

**Opened example:** Toptal reports an AI/API/UI implementation for an unnamed US media company, reducing a four-day configuration process to minutes and automating 93% of its steps. Client had 22,000+ employees; neither its identity nor project fee is disclosed. This was advanced custom engineering, not evidence a novice can duplicate it with a prompt. https://www.toptal.com/case-study/media-leader-cuts-onboarding-from-days-to-minutes-ai

**Commercial question:** distinguish a standard CRM form/workflow setup from complicated account provisioning. The former may be modest implementation; the latter needs a reliable technical team and approved scope. Same business outcome, very different delivery level.

## 6. Recruitment operations and interview coordination

**Buyer/problem:** recruiters repeatedly update interview attendance, rescheduling, hiring stage and client/team handoffs across calendars and their applicant-tracking system. This is recruiting operations, not a system making hiring decisions.

**What the business gets:** clean event-to-ATS synchronisation, interview-state tracking, missing-action prompts and a human-owned handoff into placement/onboarding. Candidate suitability decisions remain with people.

**Concrete offer direction:** “Keep interviews and candidate progress updated without recruiters rebuilding the same records across tools.”

**Opened example:** Bay Area consultancy Kunai describes n8n linking Google Calendar, Lever and related operations and reports capacity equivalent to one recruiter per ten new hires. This is an automation case; it does not establish an AI model was needed or prove a current underserved market. https://n8n.io/case-studies/kunai-part2/

**Commercial question:** whether the ATS's native integrations already solve it. Do not market AI as essential if ordinary automation is enough. This is a weaker new-offer recommendation until a buyer shows a material cross-system gap.

## 7. Content-production operations

**Buyer/problem:** a team has useful source material but production stalls between writer, designer, reviewer and publisher.

**What the business gets:** source-to-draft workflow, approved brand/subject information, assets, reviewer queue and publishing after approval. Editing quality and factual accuracy still need ownership.

**Concrete offer direction:** “Turn your team's recorded expertise into reviewed content ready to publish.” This is workflow delivery, not a promise of viral views or leads.

**Earlier inspected example:** Easy Aiz's Zapier case describes Slack voice note to content, image and WordPress draft, Slack approval, then social publishing; provider reports 100+ hours released monthly. Client unnamed and US geography not established; already in the previous research, not a newly discovered US example. https://zapier.com/blog/easy-aiz-scaled-content-creation-with-ai-and-zapier/

## What these examples actually add

The first six families change the business task: measuring plans; checking financial/purchasing documents; finding internal answers; producing commercial documents; activating accounts; coordinating recruitment. They should not be counted as more versions of a receptionist or setter. Content production is a seventh distinct family previously inspected.

Some can be delivered with existing SaaS plus implementation; others require custom work. A product case establishes capability/use, not the commercial gap for our agency. Keep acquisition/delivery complexity visible and do not call all of them beginner defaults. My current judgment: recovery remains the clearer first course example; estimating and proposal production are understandable alternatives for students with relevant industry access and technical support.
