# US AI services: expanded examples and offer directions

Researched 2 November 2026. Twenty additional case/service entries below, grouped into seven commercially useful families. These are not twenty distinct services, not twenty independently audited wins, and not a refreshed Meta scaling leaderboard. Relevant case text and offer sections were opened, rather than relying on snippets. None of the videos was watched. Most results are customer/provider accounts published by vendors; sales, bookings, attributed revenue, influenced revenue and cash collected remain different measurements.

## What this changes

The more compelling opportunity is to own a measurable business process: converting paid enquiries, recovering unsold work, filling existing-customer appointments, or operating customer support. The product is implementation plus ongoing operation, not a subscription link or an impressive custom screen.

Two particularly relevant agency models emerged. Free Agency installs lead conversion and follow-up across trades clients. Open Late Collective operates ecommerce CX with ongoing AI quality checks and refinement; its own site advertises CX optimization from $5,000 and broader fractional CX leadership from $8,000/month. Those are advertised service prices, not verified transaction values, AI-only fees or prices a novice can automatically charge.

Our previous tests overcorrected toward easy native setup. Existing software does not disqualify an offer. The question is whether a client needs valuable implementation and ongoing ownership beyond what vendor onboarding provides. Conversely, a real customer deployment does not prove a beginner can win or fulfil the same deal.

## 1. Turn paid enquiries into attended sales opportunities

**E01 — Precision AI Growth / Sleep Treatment Direct, Florida.** Agency built a Meta acquisition and appointment-setting campaign for a sleep-apnea treatment business. It reports over $18,000 contracted treatment revenue after six weeks and over $30,000 by late April 2026. These are contracted figures, not demonstrated cash or profit. The page says dedicated setter; it does not establish that setter was AI. [Source](https://precisionaigrowth.com/clientresults).

**E02 — Precision AI Growth / Sleeping Solutions & Wellness, Detroit.** Same agency describes landing page/funnel build plus Meta and Google advertising. Claims 27 new patients in 60 days, followed by 10–15 additional patients per month. Supports an agency selling an acquisition system; AI's exact role and campaign costs are unstated. [Source](https://precisionaigrowth.com/clientresults).

**E03 — Results Grow / field-service client.** Zapier describes an agency building a ChatGPT and Tables booking chatbot for a client whose leads waited up to three days. Reports $134,000+ annual revenue improvement and 30% of appointments booked by the chatbot. Named agency and operator, unnamed end client; no independent attribution or current acquisition cost. [Source](https://zapier.com/customer-stories/results-grow).

**E04 — American Standard / Regal.** Immediate response to home-remodeling enquiries, structured qualification and handoffs. Vendor reports $1.5 million in revenue from AI-led interactions in under five months. High-volume enterprise setting, not a small-contractor forecast; exact incrementality and margins unavailable. [Source](https://www.regal.ai/case-study/american-standard-o7kb3).

**Student-facing service:** connect one profitable lead source to a qualification, follow-up and booking process, then monitor attended appointments and sold work. Only add advertising management if the delivery team can manage media and creative; software alone does not generate demand. Prefer buyers already receiving enough enquiries to establish a baseline.

**Possible positioning:** “Get more booked jobs from the enquiries you're already paying for.” This is a draft direction, not an approved guarantee.

**Acquisition judgment:** a candidate for both niche Meta marketing and targeted outreach. Existing lead flow and margin are qualification requirements; campaign viability is not established by these cases.

## 2. Turn unsold estimates and old customers into additional work

**E05 — Bone Dry Roofing / Hatch.** Reports over $7 million closed from rehash and follow-up in one year, with a dedicated four-person team operating across 17 instances. Better evidence for a managed process than an autonomous bot. Large multi-state operator; no controlled counterfactual. [Source](https://www.usehatchapp.com/case-studies/bone-dry-roofing).

**E06 — Crown Roofing & Waterproofing / Hatch.** Florida contractor describes Salesforce-connected response, after-hours intake, estimate follow-up and nurture. Reports $365,000 in sales attributed to Hatch in 60 days. Attribution is not proof all revenue was incremental or collected; the vendor's 100× ROI is not independently audited. [Source](https://www.usehatchapp.com/case-studies/crown-roofing-and-waterproofing).

**E07 — Pella operations described in Hatch's East Coast case.** Past-customer recontact, quote nurture and rep-specific boards. Operator reports over 30% annual growth without extra scheduling/coordinating headcount and a six-figure whole-house sale from a recontacted buyer. The page describes a seven-state operation and a Richmond-based COO; do not claim this is all national Pella activity or AI alone caused growth. Long-running platform relationship, not evidence every historical action used present-day AI. [Source](https://www.usehatchapp.com/case-studies/pella).

**E08 — Anonymous Indiana roofing contractor / Hatch.** Consolidated 8–10 lead sources, integrated AccuLynx, and added qualification and rehash. Reports about $100,000 booked from rehash in one month. Appointments rose 400→700, which is 75%, despite the page's “doubled” headline. Unnamed company, changed first names, multi-channel activity; weaker identification than Bone Dry. [Source](https://www.usehatchapp.com/case-studies/indiana-roofing-company).

**Student-facing service:** work one eligible segment—recent unsold estimates, cancelled appointments or past buyers—using approved messaging, reply handling, scheduling and rep handoff. Reconcile completed sales against the source cohort. Do not bundle every lifecycle stage into the first pilot.

**Possible positioning:** “Turn estimates you've already sent into your next sold jobs.” The business still prices, sells and delivers the work.

**Acquisition judgment:** worth testing through niche Meta ads and outbound. Fit is an owner with substantial unused pipeline, not every business in the industry. Build an ongoing lifecycle service only after demonstrating ongoing work to manage; a finite old list does not justify an indefinite retainer by itself.

## 3. Convert after-hours and overflow demand into bookings

**E09 — Peaden / Hatch.** Florida HVAC, plumbing and electrical business integrated voice AI with ServiceTitan, supplemented by recontact and slow-season campaigns. Reports nearly triple conversion on after-hours/overflow calls and 3,600 calls handled monthly. Large existing operation; “revenue influenced” is not new cash caused solely by AI. [Source](https://www.usehatchapp.com/case-studies/peaden-voice).

**E10 — Clancy Relocation & Logistics / Regal.** Replaced an inconsistent outsourced after-hours answering service with AI qualification and structured follow-ups. Reports 96% faster answer time. Strong example of replacing existing paid work; not an audited revenue-lift result. [Source](https://www.regal.ai/case-study/clancy-relocation-logistics).

**E11 — 5 Star Dentists Sugar Land/Telfair / RevenueWell.** Current vendor page contains a named treatment-coordinator account of AI collecting missing patient information; platform also offers PMS-connected scheduling. The site's estimated appointment revenue is not case-specific collected cash. Health-data and appointment integration requirements make this more demanding than generic call forwarding. [Source](https://www.revenuewell.com/dental-ai-receptionist).

**Student-facing service:** capture one category of missed demand, use accurate availability/booking rules, escalate emergencies or complex requests, and report what became attended appointments and paid work. Saturated generic positioning can be narrowed by business workflow, scheduling integration and service ownership; do not pretend those gaps are already established.

**Possible positioning:** “Keep booking work when your office can't answer.”

**Acquisition judgment:** a plausible Meta/outbound service family with an easy demonstration, but mature direct SaaS alternatives constrain an undifferentiated setup fee.

## 4. Fill maintenance and repeat-service capacity

**E12 — Conversica's Fill Your Service Bay program.** Explicit automotive offer: connect DMS, re-engage unscheduled customers, follow up declined work and support recurring service booking. Includes onboarding, strategy and ongoing performance work. Current offered program, not a named-customer outcome case. Its October 20 cohort reference is stale on the inspection date. [Source](https://www.conversica.com/fill-your-service-bay-program).

Combine with the earlier inspected **Podium/Air Design** case: 471 members contacted, 187 jobs booked and $35,000 reported collected in six weeks. This is seasonal membership scheduling, not simply a claim that old leads have been contacted. [Earlier source](https://homeservices.podium.com/resources/case-study/air-design-ai-membership-coordinator).

**Student-facing service:** select due maintenance or eligible declined-service cohorts, offer relevant appointments, coordinate availability and leave technical advice/pricing to the team. Verify the real service need and spare capacity. Unlike a one-off dormant list, repeat-service intervals can support recurring operation.

**Possible positioning:** “Fill more service appointments from customers who already know you.”

**Acquisition judgment:** attractive to test with owners/service managers via outbound and niche Meta creative. Dealership DMS access is a higher barrier than a generic calendar; select platform support before offering integration.

## 5. Managed ecommerce AI support and sales

**E13 — Open Late Collective.** Named agency and its own service site corroborate vendor partnership. Gorgias reports one merchant reached 30–40% automation in 30 days; ongoing work includes dedicated ownership, QA and weekly refinement. Own site lists Chicago address and public service pricing. Anonymous AI merchant and varying four-month/six-month “two agents” timing mean do not turn that into a fixed promise. [Partner case](https://www.gorgias.com/partner-stories/open-late-collective), [agency service site](https://openlatecollective.com/).

**E14 — Avenue Z / Gorgias.** US agency configures conversational support as part of ecommerce growth work. Reports up to 60% tickets resolved with AI and 65% agent time released; customer base is primarily US, generally $25 million+ revenue. Evidence of agency-mediated implementation, not a novice benchmark or automatically avoided payroll. [Source](https://www.gorgias.com/partner-stories/avenue-z).

**E15 — Retailogists / Gorgias.** Agency with Montreal/New York presence says it onboarded 15+ ecommerce brands; a client achieved 29% chat conversion during BFCM. Support actions and product recommendations integrated with Shopify. North American/European mix; individual result not identified as US-only or generalizable. [Source](https://www.gorgias.com/partner-stories/retailogists).

**E16 — Pepper / Gorgias.** Named brand implements support plus shopping assistance; reports 54% average automation, 19% conversion among AI-assisted sales conversations and 18% higher AOV. These are not a 19% site-wide lift or a randomized causal result. Large brand; ticket volume drives fit. [Source](https://www.gorgias.com/customers/pepper).

**Student-facing service:** connect store/helpdesk, organise approved policies and product information, configure a narrow set of support actions, test exceptions and escalation, then monitor quality and improve weekly. A second, distinct deliverable is pre-purchase product guidance; do not confuse support deflection with sales conversion or count both as the same saving.

**Possible positioning:** “Handle more customer questions without growing your support queue.” For shopping assistance: “Help shoppers choose and buy while your team is offline.”

**Acquisition judgment:** strong specialist path with targeted ecommerce outreach, ecosystem partnerships and potentially Meta. Requires customer-service competence and ongoing management. The reason to pay an agency is accountable implementation/operation, not exclusive access to software.

## 6. Agency growth systems operated across multiple clients

**E17 — Free Agency / Hatch.** Fractional CMO service for trades, with named clients and Hatch deployment across its accounts. Reports $70 million in client revenue influenced/passed through Hatch over 12 months—not $70 million incremental AI-generated revenue or agency revenue. Their own site confirms service + software, marketing leadership and revenue reporting. This is a mature operating model, not a weekend beginner build. [Case](https://www.usehatchapp.com/case-studies/free-agency), [agency site](https://freeagency.ai/).

Related prior example **ScaleAgents** packages paid media, funnels and AI appointment setting for agencies/consultants. It is an offered service model; no audited unit economics obtained. [Source](https://scaleagents.io/).

**Student-facing lesson:** a narrow revenue-recovery or booking installation can later expand to a managed conversion system. The broad package requires separate media, creative, integration and sales competence. Do not have a novice promise all of it because a mature agency sells it.

## 7. More advanced implementations with real buyer examples

**E18 — Aprende Institute / Regal.** US/Latin America vocational education platform uses qualification and transfers to advisors, with Salesforce/Five9 integration. Reports 100,000+ advisor minutes saved monthly and conversion parity with humans. Shows why telephony, handoff engineering and ongoing support matter; much larger than a first student client. [Source](https://www.regal.ai/case-study/aprende-institute).

**E19 — a360inc / Regal.** AI contacts notaries, collects bids and sends structured results into the company's system. Useful example of staffing/dispatch/vendor coordination rather than consumer lead generation. Reports 80% lower cost per call, but the page mixes $400,000 and $1 million savings figures; neither used as a reliable combined total. [Source](https://www.regal.ai/case-study/a360).

**E20 — Southwest Recovery Services / Skit.ai.** Dallas collections operator deploys inbound/outbound voice AI; vendor reports over 400,000 automated outbound calls. This establishes an advanced implemented category, not a beginner-ready debt-chasing package. Specialized collections expertise, permitted contact practices and oversight are part of delivery; no legal suitability assessed here. [Source](https://skit.ai/resource/case-studies/every-call-answered-every-account-reached/).

## Commercial priorities for our course

My judgment, not demonstrated student conversion data:

1. **Existing-lead conversion and estimate recovery:** most direct match to a beginner explaining owner pain, showing a working conversation and selling a bounded first implementation. Need pipeline volume, eligibility, spare sales/delivery capacity and a meaningful gap.
2. **Repeat-service booking and renewal:** clear recurring operating need; strongest when backed by an actual customer list and service schedule. Avoid speculative contact-list blasting.
3. **Ads + AI booking:** compelling larger service when media/creative delivery is available. Track attended appointments, sold work and contribution, not lead count alone. Ad spend is a delivery input, not guaranteed client profit.
4. **Managed ecommerce support:** credible specialist retainer supported by actual agency examples and public service prices. Needs merchant volume and quality discipline; not universal beginner default.
5. **Advanced operations:** retain vendor coordination, education intake and collections as custom examples for experienced students/technical partners, not the starting curriculum.

These are proposed service directions, not approved guarantees. No source justifies giving a new student a made-up revenue promise. Use a functioning demo plus limited scope as initial authority, and a technically supported acceptance promise before claiming downstream financial outcomes.

## What remains unestablished

We have better deployment and service-packaging evidence, not current Meta spend/profit proof. Many strong examples are large buyers, and several come from the same vendor; this is not a representative market-size survey. No agency margins, closed prices, student acquisition costs or current reach counts are verified.

This run retried the public US `AI reactivation` Ad Library query in Safari. It remained a blank loading page on repeat inspection. No refreshed per-page counts, new creative/start-date records, or scaling labels are claimed. The earlier appointment-setter search remains partial evidence from FIRST-PASS.md. No outreach, purchase, production build, deployment or canonical course/skill changes made.

Next validation should deepen three selected service families: exact competing packages and onboarding, identified buyer sample and reachable audience, actual vendor access/costs, ordinary-software alternative, and a small buyer/pilot test. Breadth has improved; the next step is comparing commercially deliverable offers rather than accumulating more unsupported claims.
